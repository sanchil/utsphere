\begin{abstract}
% DRAFT -- tighten once H1/H2 are formalized with equations.
General relativity identifies gravity with spacetime curvature:
mass-energy curves spacetime, and free bodies follow the resulting
geodesics. This paper sets out two working hypotheses that revisit
that identification from opposite directions. Hypothesis~1 proposes
that space itself is an elastic medium, and that the amount of
curvature a given mass produces is governed by that medium's
elasticity, so that the strength of gravity is tied directly to the
elastic response of space rather than treated as a bare coupling
constant. Hypothesis~2 makes that identification the paper's defining statement
of gravity rather than a background assumption: spacetime curvature,
sourced by mass, is the cause, and the felt force called gravity is
its direct consequence -- specifically, the widening gap between the
local flow of time at neighboring points as curvature deepens between
them. Two candidate drivers for the felt sensation of weight were
checked against this definition: a radial, mass-centered one
(curvature itself, carrying real, measurable tidal curvature -- this
paper calls it ``centripetal'' as a label for that radial, curvature-
sourced term, not in the stricter textbook sense of the sideways
acceleration that holds a body on a circular path; see
\S\ref{subsec:h2} for the distinction) and a centrifugal one (a
constant rotation of space). Only the radial, curvature-sourced case
satisfies the definition; the centrifugal case reproduces the same
felt sensation, via the equivalence principle, while carrying no true
curvature at all, and is kept in this paper specifically to show what
gravity is \emph{not}. An earlier attempt to source the radial case
from cosmic expansion's own acceleration was checked numerically and
found roughly ten orders of magnitude too weak, confirming curvature
itself, not any external driver, as the right place to locate the
cause -- though, as \S\ref{subsec:h2} and \S\ref{sec:discussion} make
explicit, locating the cause this way is the Newtonian limit of the
ordinary geodesic equation, not content beyond general relativity.
Both hypotheses rest on a shared departure from the standard picture:
treating space not as an empty, static backdrop but as a real medium
with its own fluidity and elasticity. Where the analogies used to
develop that picture break against what is already established --
several demonstrably do -- this paper treats those breaks, in
general, as marking where further observational and experimental work
is needed on the medium's own properties, rather than as grounds to
abandon the picture as a whole; where a break is instead a direct
measurement against a specific claim's own stated prediction (as with
H2a's original cosmic-expansion engine, H4's literal claim, and H6's
scale claim; \S\ref{sec:discussion}), that specific claim is withdrawn
outright rather than merely flagged for later work -- the distinction
is between the research program and any one claim made under it.
Both hypotheses are stated here in their roughest form, as
working ideas to be formalized, checked against known limits, and
tested against observational constraints -- not as completed
theories. Some of general relativity's quantitative successes are
already recovered here, not merely promised: H1 reproduces
inverse-square attraction, $g=GM/r^2$, by construction
(\S\ref{subsec:h1}), and H2c's deflection split -- the standard GR
formula, transcribed rather than independently derived from this
paper's own machinery -- is evaluated numerically against the
measured solar light-bending angle of $1.75''$ and matches it
(\S\ref{subsec:h2}). Mercury's perihelion precession and
gravitational-wave speed are not yet checked against this paper's
framework; establishing those two specifically is the first item of
future work flagged throughout.

\textbf{A note on scope.} H1 and H2 remain this paper's founding pair,
but the shared foundation they rest on (\S\ref{sec:notation}) turned
out to support several further, related hypotheses as the paper
developed -- currently H1 through H9, with H1b as a flagged extension
of H1 -- each carried to the same standard: an honest tier assessment
(standard, extrapolated, or speculative; \S\ref{sec:foundations}),
and a numeric check wherever one could be run. Confidence varies
sharply across them, by design: \S\ref{sec:foundations} and
\S\ref{sec:discussion} state plainly which are kept as live,
developed claims (H2a, revised -- gravity as the felt consequence of
spacetime curvature, this paper's defining statement, with the GPS
correction, Chou et al.'s clock data, and Earth's tidal gradient all
checked against it -- though, as noted where it is derived, these are
three numerical manifestations of the same single, already-standard
potential $\Phi=-GM/r$ for Earth, not three independent confirmations
of anything beyond it; H1, scoped; H7, without its extraction claim;
H8; H9, as an adopted and already-bounded existing program) and which are
better read as retired or substantially narrowed (H2a's original
cosmic-expansion engine, specifically, superseded by its own revision
rather than needed; H4's literal claim; H6's claim that its mechanism
reaches the scale it was built to explain) -- so a reader is never
left to guess which of the nine the paper is actually prepared to
stand on.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Summary.}
This paper starts from a single departure from the standard picture:
treat space not as an inert backdrop that mass happens to curve, but
as a real medium with its own elasticity and fluidity, properties
to be measured rather than assumed away (H1, H1b). Under that
picture, gravity itself is reframed as the felt consequence of
curvature rather than an added force: the widening gap between the
local rate of clock flow at neighboring points, as mass-sourced
curvature between them deepens, \emph{is} the acceleration -- a
restatement of the Newtonian limit of general relativity's own
geodesic equation, not a mechanism beyond it (H2a), with a spatial
bend alone shown to be too weak, and a flat-spacetime rotational
alternative shown to carry no true curvature, to do the job on their
own (H2b, H2c). The same departure is then pressed against a cluster
of standing astrophysical puzzles -- what seeds the largest black
holes, what seeds ordinary accretion, why galaxies rotate as they do
without invoking dark matter, where energy goes as space expands or
compresses, what a collapsing core actually becomes, and why gravity
is so much weaker than every other force (H3--H9) -- not to replace
general relativity, but to see how far ``space as a real medium''
can be pushed before the numbers themselves say otherwise. Several
of these hypotheses do not survive their own numeric check and are
withdrawn here rather than argued around (\S\ref{sec:discussion});
what remains is offered as a disciplined set of working hypotheses,
each stated plainly enough to be falsified, not as a finished
alternative to general relativity.

\emph{Treating space as an empty, static backdrop to events --

is this paper's central departure from the standard picture, and is
proposed here as a more fruitful place to locate the open problems

outright. But where a specific claim built on the picture is checked

\paragraph{Working postulates.}
% Stated explicitly as assumptions this paper adopts as its starting
% point -- to be judged by what they let the paper correctly predict,
% not asserted here as already-established fact. Stating them plainly,
% up front, is better practice than leaving them implicit in the
% hypotheses' language.
This paper's hypotheses share three working postulates about the
nature of space, stated here explicitly so the reader can see exactly
what is being assumed before any result is derived from it.

\begin{description}
  \item[P1 (space as a medium).] Space is not merely a bookkeeping
    device for the distances between objects; it possesses real
    structure of its own. This is not an unprecedented claim: quantum
    field theory already treats the vacuum as having real energy and
    structure, confirmed in the laboratory via the Casimir effect
    (\S\ref{subsec:core_terms}). \textbf{Qualification, stated as
    plainly as the postulate itself:} P1 does \emph{not} reintroduce
    a preferred, detectable rest frame for this medium. The
    19th-century aether hypothesis proposed exactly such a frame, and
    it was ruled out by the Michelson--Morley experiment and by over
    a century of increasingly precise successors, now constraining
    any such effect to below roughly one part in $10^{17}$
    (\S\ref{subsec:h1}, \S\ref{subsec:h7} discussion). The medium P1
    refers to is modeled on the quantum vacuum's energy and
    structure -- the same in every inertial frame -- not on the
    mechanical, frame-bearing aether that experiment has already
    excluded. Any development of P1 that reintroduces a detectable
    preferred frame takes on the burden of directly answering that
    body of null results; this paper does not do so, and flags this
    as the single most important constraint on how P1 may be
    developed further.
  \item[P2 (fluidity and elasticity).] This medium is not rigid or
    inert: it is capable of expansion, compression, shear, and
    rotation (\emph{fluidity}, formalized as the kinematic triplet
    $(\theta,\sigma_{\mu\nu},\omega_{\mu\nu})$), and it resists
    bending by an amount that need not be assumed constant
    (\emph{elasticity}, formalized as the compliance field $\Sigma$).
    Both are defined precisely in \S\ref{subsec:core_terms}.
    \textbf{On the fluid analogy's proper scope.} ``Fluid'' should not
    be read as choosing between a continuum (liquid-like) picture and
    a particle (gas-like) one -- quantum fluids are not required to
    pick one. Superfluid helium is simultaneously a continuous,
    frictionless medium at long wavelength \emph{and} a gas of
    discrete, countable quasiparticles (phonons) at the level of its
    own excitations; this is exactly why superfluids and
    Bose-Einstein condensates are used as laboratory analogues for
    curved spacetime (\S\ref{sec:foundations}, analogue gravity). If
    space is a real medium with quantized excitations, it should be
    expected to show the same duality light already shows: a
    gravitational wave as the continuum description, a graviton as
    its particle counterpart -- the still-undetected, but mainstream
    and actively pursued, quantum of the gravitational field. Whether
    space genuinely has this structure remains (c) open; that a
    quantized medium \emph{can} have it simultaneously is (a)
    established physics, not a metaphor stretched to fit.
  \item[P3 (inconsistency).] This medium is not perfectly smooth: it
    admits departures from smoothness, formalized as the irregularity
    scalar $\mathcal{I}$ (\S\ref{subsec:core_terms}) and classified by
    species in the taxonomy of \S\ref{subsec:taxonomy} -- holes,
    tears, cracks, faults, channels, and valleys, alongside the
    classical spherical black hole. The qualitative picture this
    draws on is the observed cosmic web, where matter is seen
    gathering along filaments and voids rather than sitting uniformly
    distributed, much as water gathers into rivers and lakes along
    the low places in a landscape. \textbf{Qualification:} this
    qualitative resemblance is not yet independent evidence for P3.
    The cosmic web's filamentary structure already has a complete,
    quantitative, independently confirmed explanation -- gravitational
    instability acting on the density fluctuations seen in the CMB,
    growing under cold dark matter, reproduced in detail by simulation
    (\S\ref{subsec:h6} discussion). For P3 to add genuine content
    here, rather than relabel an explained phenomenon, it needs to
    reproduce the same filament and void statistics from $\mathcal{I}$
    and the kinematic triplet directly -- the open item already
    flagged in the taxonomy table's \emph{Crack/channel} row.
\end{description}

\paragraph{An illustrative picture for P1--P3.}
A useful way to hold P1--P3 in mind, borrowed from a standard
cosmological teaching device, is the surface of an expanding soap
bubble or balloon. The thin film stands in for our 3-dimensional
space; its own expansion stands in for cosmic expansion; and its
embedding in a further dimension, used purely to visualize that
expansion without needing a center, stands in for the medium-like
structure P1 proposes space itself has. Viewed from \emph{within} the
film -- the only vantage any observer in it actually has -- every
point sees every other point receding, symmetrically, with no special
point singled out as the source of the expansion; this is also
exactly what real observers see from any real galaxy, in any
direction, and is the central lesson the analogy is built to convey.
Because light has had a finite time (on the order of 13.8 billion
years) to travel since the hot Big Bang, any observer within the film
sees only a limited patch of it -- the true extent of the full film,
and of the full universe, remains unseen and may be far larger than
what is visible.

\textbf{One correction the analogy is specifically built to enforce,
not to blur:} there is no Big Bang \emph{point}, in the film or in the
embedding dimension. The embedding direction (here identified with
time) does not locate a position somewhere else in space that the
expansion came \emph{from} -- it locates a \emph{moment}: the limiting
case, run backward, where the film's radius shrinks to zero and every
point presently in the universe coincides at once. The Big Bang
happened everywhere in space, simultaneously, at one moment in the
past common to every point -- not at one place, reachable in some
direction, even an extra-dimensional one. Treated this way, the
analogy is a correct, standard illustration of P1--P3's picture of
space, rather than a restatement of the single most common
misconception the analogy was devised to correct.

Every hypothesis in \S\ref{sec:hypotheses} is best read as a specific
proposal for \emph{how} P1--P3 cash out quantitatively, not as
independent claims -- H1 proposes a form for $\Sigma$; H2, H5, and H6
propose sources and consequences for the kinematic triplet; H3, H4,
and H5 propose what $\mathcal{I}$ looks like at different scales; H7
proposes what P2's dynamics exchange with real, created energy. Judged
on their own, P1--P3 are postulates, neither true nor false until
their consequences are worked out and checked; judged together with
the hypotheses that follow, they are the common root the taxonomy
table (\S\ref{subsec:taxonomy}) and the open items throughout
\S\ref{sec:hypotheses} are asking to be filled in.

\paragraph{A guiding conviction.}
Beyond P1--P3 as stated technical postulates, this paper is written
from a conviction held by its author: that treating space as empty
nothingness, with geometry alone doing every explanatory job, is a
deeper source of several of the open problems surveyed in this paper
-- not only the ones P1--P3 touch most directly -- and that treating
space instead as a real, fluid, elastic substance is more likely to
resolve those problems than to create new ones. This conviction is
the motivation behind the choice of postulates and hypotheses in this
paper; it is stated plainly here \emph{as} a conviction, not smuggled
in as an established result, because the two are easy to conflate and
should not be. A guiding conviction earns its place in physics the
same way every idea in this paper has been asked to: by being turned
into specific, checkable claims and surviving contact with what is
already measured -- which is the task the rest of this paper, and the
open items left throughout it, are still working through rather than
a step this paragraph can shortcut.

\section{Foundational Basis and Prior Work}
\label{sec:foundations}
% =====================================================================
% For EACH hypothesis, this section should eventually contain a short,
% honest assessment of how strongly it rests on established physics.
% Suggested sub-structure per hypothesis (duplicate as needed):

\subsection{Established results this paper relies on}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Equivalence principle} (Einstein, 1907/1911; tested to
    high precision by E\"otv\"os-type and modern torsion-balance
    experiments): locally and instantaneously, a uniformly accelerated
    frame is indistinguishable from a uniform gravitational field. H2's
    original engine used this unchanged, but extended its scope from a
    local, instantaneous statement to a claim about the global
    \emph{origin} of gravity -- an extension that the magnitude checks
    in \S\ref{subsec:h2} rule out, and that engine is retired for
    exactly that reason. H2-revised, kept, does not make this
    extension at all: it uses the equivalence principle only in its
    ordinary, local scope, as the standard justification for treating
    $g_{00}$'s gradient as the gravitational acceleration.
  \item \textbf{Einstein field equations}, $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}/c^4$:
    curvature is set by mass-energy through the fixed constant $G$. H1
    keeps this structure but reinterprets $G$ (or the coupling it
    plays) as a measurable elasticity rather than a bare constant --
    a reinterpretation of an existing equation, not a new one, until
    ``elasticity'' is given independent empirical content.
  \item \textbf{Measured constancy of $G$}: Newton's constant is
    measured to be constant across laboratory tests to good precision.
    Any version of H1 in which elasticity varies must explain why that
    variation has not already shown up in existing $G$ measurements.
  \item \textbf{Cosmic expansion and its acceleration} (Hubble's law;
    the accelerating expansion inferred from Type Ia supernovae,
    Riess et al. 1998, Perlmutter et al. 1999): real, but measured to
    be extremely small and essentially uniform on the relevant scales
    -- not currently known to be locally concentrated around
    individual masses the way H2a would need.
  \item \textbf{Observed isotropy of the cosmic microwave background}:
    once the known dipole (from our own solar system's motion,
    $\sim$368 km/s) is subtracted, the residual anisotropy is at the
    $\Delta T/T\sim10^{-5}$ level. This bounds a specific kind of
    claim very tightly -- a single, coherent, \emph{global} rotation
    or directional stretching of the universe as a whole, fit via
    Bianchi-type anisotropic cosmological models against this
    residual (e.g. Jaffe et al., using WMAP), giving roughly
    $\omega/H_0\lesssim10^{-9}$. It does \emph{not} bound local,
    patchy inhomogeneity -- the $10^{-5}$ residual itself \emph{is}
    exactly that local texture, and is the measured seed of all
    structure. Directly bearing on H2b, whose claim (a single,
    universe-wide spin) is precisely the global kind this bound
    constrains; does not by itself bear on P3's local-irregularity
    premise or on a patchy (rather than globally directional) reading
    of H5.
  \item \textbf{Smoothness (differentiability) of the spacetime
    manifold}: a foundational assumption of classical GR; a literal
    topology change (a ``tear'') is excluded by it. Bears directly on
    H3/H4.
  \item \textbf{Mass bound on stellar-collapse black holes}
    (progenitor mass limits; the pair-instability mass gap, roughly
    $50$--$150\,M_\odot$): well established, and correctly used as the
    starting observation for H3.
  \item \textbf{Observed supermassive black holes at high redshift}:
    the ``seed problem'' -- a genuine, open question H3 correctly
    identifies, already addressed in the literature by direct-collapse,
    heavy-seed, and super-Eddington-accretion proposals.
  \item \textbf{Helioseismology, terrestrial seismology, and
    planetary geodesy}: direct observational probes of the interiors
    of the Sun, Earth, and other bodies, bearing directly on H4's
    claim that a black hole sits at the center of every body.
  \item \textbf{Quantum foam} (Wheeler, 1955) and \textbf{primordial
    black holes} (Zel'dovich \& Novikov, 1966; Hawking, 1971): existing,
    legitimate research programs nearest in spirit to H3/H4's
    ``inconsistencies in space,'' discussed below.
  \item \textbf{Homogeneity and isotropy of cosmic expansion (the FLRW
    model)}: supported by CMB isotropy and large-scale-structure
    surveys; the expansion rate is a single scale factor, not a
    directional flow field with distinct colliding regions. This is a
    statement about the universe's \emph{global} expansion having no
    preferred direction or coherent flow pattern -- it is fully
    compatible with, and indeed requires, the same local $10^{-5}$-level
    density patchiness noted above. Bears directly on H5's specific
    claim of large, coherently \emph{directional} colliding regions; a
    reading of H5/H3 built instead on scattered, local patchiness
    (consistent with P3) is not what this bound constrains, and would
    need to be checked instead against GR's smoothness assumption
    (next item) and against whether the known $10^{-5}$ perturbations,
    via ordinary gravitational instability, already account for the
    structure in question.
  \item \textbf{Black hole rotation, frame dragging, and
    analogue-gravity vortex experiments} (Kerr metric; Lense--Thirring
    effect, now measured via accretion-disk spin diagnostics; Unruh,
    1981, and later draining-fluid-vortex demonstrations of
    superradiance): the genuine, if indirect, point of contact for
    H5's vortex picture.
  \item \textbf{Galaxy rotation curves} (Rubin \& Ford, 1970s onward):
    the original, and still central, dark-matter evidence H6 targets.
  \item \textbf{Independent dark-matter evidence beyond rotation
    curves}: the Bullet Cluster's lensing/gas offset; the relative
    heights of the CMB acoustic peaks; the growth rate of large-scale
    structure. Each is logically independent of rotation curves and
    must be addressed by any replacement, not just the curves
    themselves.
  \item \textbf{Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND)} (Milgrom, 1983):
    the existing, well-studied alternative to dark matter closest in
    spirit to H6, with known successes (individual rotation curves)
    and known difficulties (cluster scales, the Bullet Cluster, the
    CMB).
  \item \textbf{Cosmological particle creation} (Parker, 1968),
    \textbf{inflationary reheating}, the \textbf{dynamical Casimir
    effect} (Wilson et al., 2011), and \textbf{Hawking radiation}
    (Hawking, 1974): four established mechanisms by which a changing
    spacetime or vacuum boundary converts vacuum fluctuations into
    real particles -- the direct precedent for, and current scope of,
    H7.
  \item \textbf{The Chandrasekhar and Tolman--Oppenheimer--Volkoff
    limits}; \textbf{the river model of gravity} (Painlev\'e,
    1921; Gullstrand, 1922); \textbf{the interior Schwarzschild
    solution's space/time coordinate swap}; and \textbf{Planck
    stars} (Rovelli \& Vidotto, 2014) and \textbf{fuzzballs} (Mathur,
    2005): five established or seriously-studied results underlying
    H8, covering, respectively, why collapse becomes unstoppable,
    how space ``flows into'' a mass, why infall resembles motion
    through time, and what might replace a literal singularity.
  \item \textbf{The hierarchy problem} (gravity is measured to be
    roughly $10^{36}$ times weaker than electromagnetism between two
    protons, with no settled explanation for the gap), \textbf{large
    extra-dimension (braneworld) models} (Arkani-Hamed, Dimopoulos \&
    Dvali, 1998; Randall \& Sundrum, 1999), and the \textbf{sub-
    millimeter torsion-balance tests of Newton's inverse-square law}
    (the E\"ot-Wash experiments) that already bound any such extra
    dimension to below roughly $40$--$50\,\mu\mathrm{m}$: the real,
    quantified starting point and the real, quantified constraint for
    H9.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Where each hypothesis is standard, extrapolated, or speculative}
\textbf{H1 (elasticity of space), scoped version.} The claim that
mass-energy curves space, and that the amount of curvature follows
from a fixed coupling, is (a) standard GR. The claim that this
coupling should be understood as a physical \emph{elasticity} is (b)
an extrapolation: it has real precedent as an analogy (spacetime-as-
elastic-medium pictures appear in induced-gravity and analogue-gravity
literature, \S\ref{sec:foundations}.3 below) but is not itself an
established, independently measured quantity. As now scoped
(\S\ref{subsec:h1}) -- $\Sigma$ held constant, bend computed from
mass and distance via the shell theorem, and the question of
\emph{why} bending happens explicitly deferred to H2 -- H1's
defensible core is (a) standard: it is a direct, checked recovery of
Newtonian surface gravity (confirmed numerically for Earth, Mars, the
Sun, Jupiter, and Saturn) in elasticity vocabulary, with no claimed
deviation from $G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}/c^4$. It remains (c),
speculative and currently unfalsifiable on its own, only in the
direction it has not yet been developed: a specific, independently
measurable variation of $\Sigma$ (provisionally H1b, tied to the
irregularity scalar $\mathcal{I}$) would be needed before H1 makes a
prediction standard GR does not already make.

\textbf{H1b (variable elasticity tied to local field conditions).}
That a locally-responsive coupling could restore the shape-sensitivity
a constant $\Sigma$ forbids is (b) a reasonable extrapolation of H1 --
it is the one lever within this paper's own formalism that could do
so without contradicting anything already confirmed about $\Sigma$
not differing between bodies. As stated, with no function $h$ and no
coupling $\lambda$ specified, it is (c) fully speculative and not yet
falsifiable. It inherits a real, numerical constraint directly from
its nearest precedent regardless: scalar-tensor gravity's existing
solar-system bound ($\omega>40{,}000$) already tells us how small any
such effect must be wherever $\mathcal{I}$ is small, which is a
concrete, borrowed falsifiability anchor even before H1b specifies
its own function.

\textbf{H2 (gravity sourced by expansion or spin) -- original framing,
since revised.} This assessment applies to H2's \emph{original}
statement, kept here for the record of why it was revised rather than
as the hypothesis's current form (see \S\ref{subsec:h2} for the
revision this paper now adopts as its defining statement of gravity).
The equivalence principle itself is (a) standard and extremely well
tested. Using it to say that \emph{a} sustained acceleration feels
like gravity is correct. Using it to say that cosmic expansion or
cosmic rotation \emph{is the acceleration that constitutes all
gravity, everywhere, including the mass-centered field around the
Earth} is (c) a new postulate with no direct precedent and, as
detailed in \S\ref{subsec:h2}, was in tension with the measured size
and uniformity of cosmic expansion, with CMB-isotropy bounds on
cosmic rotation, and with the basic mass-centered, inverse-square
shape of every gravitational field actually observed. This was the
weakest part of either hypothesis in its original form, precisely
because it was the most novel claim and the one most directly checked
against existing, precise observations -- which is exactly why H2a
was revised to locate the cause in curvature itself rather than in an
external driver, removing the objection rather than carrying it
forward.

\textbf{H3 (tears as the origin of supermassive black holes).} That
stellar-collapse black holes are mass-limited, and that supermassive
black holes pose an unsolved seed problem, is (a) standard and
accurate. That the resolution is a literal tear in spacetime's fabric
is (c) a new postulate: GR's manifold is smooth by construction, so
this is not an extrapolation of an existing pattern but a proposed
exception to a foundational assumption, and it currently has to
compete with, rather than build on, the mainstream candidate answers
(direct collapse, heavy seeds, super-Eddington growth) without yet
stating what distinguishes it from them observationally.

\textbf{H4 (small inconsistencies seeding all accretion; a black hole
at the center of every body).} That accretion builds stars, planets,
and asteroids, and that growing mass deepens a gravitational well, is
(a) standard accretion physics, just stated in this paper's own
vocabulary. That the \emph{trigger} for accretion is a small tear or
hole in space, rather than ordinary gravitational instability in a
gas or dust cloud, is (c) a new, currently unnecessary postulate --
the mainstream mechanism already accounts for the observation without
it. The claim that every body has a black hole at its center is also
(c), and is the most strongly constrained part of either hypothesis:
it is checked directly against existing interior-structure data
(helioseismology, seismology, geodesy) and, as stated without
qualification, does not currently match it.

\textbf{H5 (supermassive black holes as colliding-space vortices).}
The starting observation -- that a single star's collapse cannot
directly produce a supermassive black hole -- is (a) standard and
correctly stated. The vortex/rotation imagery has (b) a real,
extrapolatable point of contact: real black holes do drag spacetime
when they rotate, and that rotational mathematics is exactly what
analogue-gravity vortex experiments exploit. But the specific sourcing
mechanism -- large regions of space expanding in opposite directions
and colliding -- is (c) a new postulate with no observational
counterpart in the standard, homogeneous-and-isotropic picture of
cosmic expansion, and it has to explain away, not just outpace, the
mainstream seed-growth picture (stellar-mass seeds plus mergers and
sustained accretion), which already accounts for supermassive black
holes' observed mass and spin without requiring colliding expansion.

\textbf{H6 (galaxies as vortex depressions, replacing dark matter).}
The target observation -- flat rotation curves -- is (a) standard and
correctly identified. Attributing it to the central vortex (the
galaxy's supermassive black hole) is (c) a new postulate that, as it
stands, faces a direct scale problem: the black hole's gravitational
reach is three to four orders of magnitude smaller than the radii
where flat rotation curves are actually measured, so the mechanism as
stated does not yet reach the regime it is meant to explain. It is
also (c) silent on the independent, non-rotation-curve evidence for
dark matter (Bullet Cluster, CMB acoustic peaks, structure growth),
each of which would need its own account. This is the most
empirically consequential hypothesis in the set so far, precisely
because dark matter is supported by several independent lines of
evidence rather than one -- a replacement has to clear all of them,
not just the one it was built to match.

\textbf{H7 (energy from expansion/compression of space).} This is
the best-grounded of the seven: the general phenomenon -- a changing
spacetime or vacuum boundary converting vacuum fluctuations into real
particles -- is (a) standard, established physics in four separate,
serious contexts (cosmological particle creation, inflationary
reheating, the dynamical Casimir effect, Hawking radiation). Treating
these four as instances of one general principle tied to $\theta$ is
(b) a reasonable extrapolation -- a unification claim, not a new
physical mechanism. Where H7 becomes (c), a new and much harder
claim, is in the inference that this energy ``should be harnessed'':
every established instance is a conversion process with a specific,
identifiable cost paid elsewhere (the black hole's own mass, the
energy used to drive a boundary at relativistic speed, the inflaton
field's energy), not a net source, so the extraction claim is not an
extrapolation of the established pattern but a departure from it,
and currently has no proposed mechanism stating what would pay its
cost.

\textbf{H8 (black hole formation as a core sinking into time).} This
is the best-grounded hypothesis in the set. ``Too heavy to be
supported'' is (a) standard stellar-collapse physics outright. ``Space
flows into the mass, carrying matter with it'' is (a) a correct
restatement of the already-standard river model. ``Infall resembles
motion through time'' is (a) a precise, correct description of a
known feature of the Schwarzschild interior, not a metaphor stretched
past its proper use. ``A finite core, not a literal point'' is (b), at
best a specific proposal competing with -- or possibly a restatement
of -- existing, actively studied quantum-gravity candidates (Planck
stars, fuzzballs) for the same open question, rather than a wholly
new claim. The one piece that is (c), new and currently unaddressed,
is the vortex: nothing about sinking by itself, with no stated
angular momentum, produces rotation in the river-model picture, so
H8 needs to add that ingredient explicitly rather than treat the
vortex as automatic.

\textbf{H9 (gravitational leakage into a hidden dimension).} The
starting observation -- gravity is roughly $10^{36}$ times weaker
than electromagnetism, with no settled explanation -- is (a) a real,
established, unsolved puzzle (the hierarchy problem). That this
weakness could be dilution into an extra spatial dimension is (b) a
direct extrapolation of existing, actively studied large-extra-
dimension models (Arkani-Hamed, Dimopoulos \& Dvali, 1998; Randall \&
Sundrum, 1999), not a new mechanism invented for this paper. As
stated, with no value given for $n$ (the number of extra dimensions)
or $\ell_\ast$ (the fundamental length scale), H9 is (c) not yet
falsifiable on its own terms, and it is already significantly
constrained by existing data regardless of how it is specified: sub-
millimeter torsion-balance tests already bound any such dimension to
below $\sim$40--50 $\mu$m, which rules out the more dramatic readings
of ``huge in 4D'' and leaves only a small, tightly bounded version
available.

\subsection{Relation to existing programs}
\textbf{H1} sits closest to \emph{induced gravity} (Sakharov, 1967),
where $G$ itself is proposed to emerge from vacuum fluctuations rather
than being fundamental, and to various \emph{analogue-gravity}
treatments that model spacetime's response to mass with
elastic-medium mathematics as a teaching or effective-theory device.
These analogue treatments are not chosen arbitrarily: \emph{superfluid
vacuum theory} and related programs use quantum fluids (superfluid
helium, Bose-Einstein condensates) specifically because such fluids
are simultaneously a continuum medium \emph{and} a gas of quantized
excitations (phonons) -- the same duality light shows as a
wave/photon pair, and the same duality a quantized gravitational field
would show as a gravitational-wave/graviton pair, were gravity's
quantization ever confirmed. This sharpens, but does not resolve,
H1's elasticity claim: it says a real quantum medium \emph{can}
consistently be both fluid-like and particle-like at once, not that
space \emph{is} such a medium.
Verlinde's entropic-gravity proposal (2011) is a different but related
example of treating gravity as emergent from a coarser underlying
layer rather than fundamental. None of these establish that spacetime
literally possesses a material elastic modulus; they are precedents
for the \emph{style} of H1's claim, not confirmations of it.
\textbf{H1b} specifically sits closest to \emph{scalar-tensor gravity}
(Brans--Dicke, 1961), which already promotes $G$ to a dynamical field
and is tightly bounded in the solar system ($\omega>40{,}000$ via
Cassini Shapiro-delay tracking, Bertotti, Iess \& Tortora 2003) --
the nearest existing, numerically precise precedent for how small any
field-dependent variation in $\Sigma$ must already be wherever it has
been tested; and, for the style of a locally-triggered departure from
constant coupling, to \emph{MOND} (Milgrom, 1983, \S\ref{sec:foundations}.3
below), though MOND's trigger is a universal acceleration scale, not
the irregularity scalar $\mathcal{I}$.

\textbf{H2's} ``sinking into space'' picture has a rigorous existing
counterpart worth knowing about: the \emph{river model of gravity}
(Painlev\'e, 1921; Gullstrand, 1922; popularized by Hamilton \& Lisle,
2008), in which space itself is pictured as flowing inward toward a
mass -- mathematically just a coordinate choice (Painlev\'e--Gullstrand
coordinates) fully equivalent to standard Schwarzschild/GR, not a
modification of it. That model gives ``mass makes space flow/sink''
a precise, already-solved form, but notably sources the flow from the
\emph{local} mass, not from cosmic expansion or universal spin -- the
opposite of what H2a/H2b propose. A genuine, non-trivial rotating
universe is also an existing GR solution (G\"odel, 1949), but it is
non-expanding, permits closed timelike curves, and is not considered a
model of our universe; current observations bound our universe's
actual rotation far below what H2b would require. Distinguishing
H2 from the river model -- i.e. stating clearly whether the ``sinking''
is sourced locally (as in the river model) or globally (as currently
written) -- is the single most useful next step for sharpening H2.

\textbf{H3 and H4} sit closest to two existing, separate programs that
this paper's single notion of ``inconsistency'' currently blends
together. \emph{Quantum foam} (Wheeler, 1955) proposes that spacetime
is topologically non-trivial at the Planck scale due to quantum
fluctuations -- a real precedent for ``space is not smooth,'' but at a
scale enormously below anything relevant to black-hole formation, with
no established bridge to that scale. \emph{Primordial black holes}
(Zel'dovich \& Novikov, 1966; Hawking, 1971) propose that density
fluctuations in the early universe collapse directly into black holes
of a wide range of masses, independent of stellar collapse, and have
been studied both as dark-matter candidates and as possible seeds for
structure formation -- a real precedent for ``small holes seed
accretion'' and even for ``some black holes are not stellar remnants,''
but sourced by early-universe density fluctuations, not by tears from
cosmic expansion or turbulence. Reframing H3/H4 explicitly in terms of
one of these two existing programs -- rather than the new term
``inconsistency,'' which currently has no agreed definition in either
GR or quantum gravity -- would let the hypotheses inherit an existing,
worked-out mathematical machinery instead of having to build one from
scratch.

\textbf{H5} sits closest to two existing, separate programs as well.
\emph{Analogue gravity} (Unruh, 1981, and subsequent draining-vortex
experiments) shows that a real fluid vortex can mathematically mimic
a rotating black hole's horizon and ergosphere for waves travelling
in that fluid -- a precedent for the mathematics of a ``vortex,'' but
one built because sound waves in a draining fluid obey the same wave
equation as fields near a Kerr horizon, not because spacetime itself
is a fluid that forms vortices. \emph{Bubble-collision black-hole
formation} during an early-universe phase transition (studied in
certain inflationary and phase-transition cosmological models) is the
closest precedent for ``a collision forms a black hole,'' but it is a
mechanism specific to the very early universe and to a particular
particle-physics scenario, not a general or ongoing process tied to
cosmic expansion as such.

A third, more specific precedent is worth adding alongside these two:
\emph{brane cosmology}, and in particular the \emph{ekpyrotic/cyclic
universe} models (Khoury, Ovrut, Seiberg, Steinhardt, 2001; Steinhardt
\& Turok, 2002), in which our observable universe is a
3-dimensional ``brane'' embedded in a higher-dimensional bulk, and the
hot Big Bang itself is proposed to be the literal collision of our
brane with another one moving through that extra dimension. This is
the closest existing precedent to ``colliding space'' taken
literally, as a worked, higher-dimensional mechanism rather than a
metaphor -- note that it requires a dimension \emph{beyond} ordinary
4-dimensional spacetime (3 space, 1 time), not simply a fourth
dimension, since spacetime already has one. Two caveats temper it as
support for H5 specifically: (i) it is a mechanism proposed for a
single, universe-defining event -- the origin of the Big Bang itself
-- not a repeating, local process that could separately spin up a
vortex at the center of each galaxy over the course of cosmic history,
so H5 would need its own argument for extending a one-time origin
mechanism into a recurring, local one; and (ii) ekpyrotic/cyclic
models remain a minority position within cosmology, disfavored by
some researchers relative to standard inflation, in part over specific
predictions (such as the pattern of primordial gravitational waves)
that current data has not decisively settled either way.

As with H3/H4, stating which of these three
programs H5 means to extend -- and on what grounds its collision
mechanism applies well beyond the regime (early-universe-origin, or
a fluid-analogy for waves) where its nearest precedents are actually
set -- is the clearest way to sharpen it.

\textbf{H6} sits closest to \emph{MOND} and its relativistic
extensions (TeVeS and successors), which already pursue exactly H6's
stated goal -- explaining rotation curves without a new matter
species -- by modifying the force law or the field equations at low
accelerations, rather than by invoking a vortex. MOND's real track
record is informative either way: it fits individual galaxy rotation
curves remarkably well, which is evidence that \emph{some}
modification-of-gravity approach can work at that scale: but it
has never comfortably accounted for the Bullet Cluster or the CMB
acoustic-peak ratios without reintroducing some form of unseen mass.
If H6 is pursued further, inheriting MOND's success on rotation
curves is the easy part; it will need its own answer to the harder
part MOND has not solved, rather than treating rotation curves as
the whole problem.

\textbf{H7} does not need a new precedent -- it names four that
already exist and already agree with one another: cosmological
particle creation (Parker, 1968) and inflationary reheating describe
expansion converting vacuum/field energy into real particles;
the dynamical Casimir effect demonstrates the same conversion from a
moving boundary in the laboratory; Hawking radiation demonstrates it
from a compressing (collapsing) geometry. H7's actual contribution,
if developed, would be showing these four are special cases of one
relation involving $\theta$ -- a worthwhile, well-posed piece of
theoretical unification -- provided it stops there, at the
unification claim, rather than extending to net extraction, which
none of its four precedents support.

\textbf{H9} sits directly on top of two existing, closely related
programs: the \textbf{ADD model} (Arkani-Hamed, Dimopoulos \& Dvali,
1998), in which gravity's weakness is explained by its spreading into
$n$ large extra dimensions while the other forces stay confined to
our 4D brane, and the \textbf{Randall--Sundrum model} (1999), which
achieves a similar dilution with a warped, rather than flat, extra
dimension. Both were developed specifically to address the hierarchy
problem H9 starts from, so H9 is not proposing a new mechanism, only
adopting an existing one and connecting it to this paper's own
language ($\Sigma_0$ as the diluted, 4D-effective elasticity). Both
parent models are already tightly constrained by sub-millimeter
gravity tests and collider searches for missing-energy gravitons,
and H9 inherits those constraints directly rather than starting from
a clean slate.

\subsection{Core Terms: Elasticity and Fluidity of Space}
\label{subsec:core_terms}
Hypotheses H1--H6 all lean on two intuitive properties of space --
its \emph{elasticity} (how much it resists bending) and its
\emph{fluidity} (how it flows, expands, spins, or shears locally).
Rather than inventing new mathematics for these, each is defined here
as a generalization of an object GR already has, so that ``standard
GR'' is the special case where the generalization is held fixed, and
each hypothesis's new content is exactly the claim that it isn't.

\paragraph{Elasticity: the compliance field $\Sigma$.}
Standard GR writes $G_{\mu\nu} = (8\pi G/c^4)\,T_{\mu\nu}$, a fixed
coupling. The combination $c^4/(8\pi G) \approx 4.98\times10^{42}\,
\mathrm{N}$ is already informally called the \emph{stiffness of
spacetime} (Thorne and others) -- it is how much stress-energy it
takes to produce a given amount of curvature, i.e. exactly the
quantity H1 calls elasticity, already present in the theory as a
constant. Define
\begin{equation}
\Sigma(x) \;:=\; \frac{c^4}{8\pi G(x)}, \qquad
G_{\mu\nu} \;=\; \frac{1}{\Sigma(x)}\,T_{\mu\nu}.
\end{equation}
Standard GR is the case $\Sigma(x) = \Sigma_0$, constant everywhere.
\textbf{H1's content is exactly, and only, the claim that $\Sigma$
varies} -- with density, curvature, scale, or something else to be
specified -- subject to reducing to the measured constant $\Sigma_0$
everywhere $G$ has actually been tested (\S\ref{subsec:h1}). Writing
$\Sigma(x)$ rather than ``elasticity'' turns H1 into a statement about
one specific field, with one specific job: say what $\Sigma$ depends
on, and by how much, and the hypothesis becomes checkable.

\paragraph{Fluidity: the kinematic triplet $(\theta, \sigma_{\mu\nu},
\omega_{\mu\nu})$.}
GR already has a standard, rigorous way to describe a ``flow'' of
space: take a congruence of worldlines (e.g. the worldlines of
observers at rest with respect to local matter) and decompose how
nearby worldlines move relative to one another into three pieces --
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\theta$, the \textbf{expansion scalar}: the local
    rate at which a small volume of worldlines grows or shrinks.
    This is precisely cosmic expansion, generalized to be local: in
    the standard cosmological model $\theta = 3H$, three times the
    Hubble parameter. \textbf{H2a's ``expanding space''} is a
    statement about $\theta$.
  \item $\omega_{\mu\nu}$, the \textbf{vorticity (rotation) tensor}:
    the local rotation of nearby worldlines about each other.
    \textbf{H2b's ``spin of space''} and the rotational character of
    \textbf{H5's and H6's ``vortex''} are statements about
    $\omega_{\mu\nu}$ (or its vector form $\omega^\mu$).
  \item $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$, the \textbf{shear tensor}: local
    stretching or squeezing without a change in volume or net
    rotation -- distortion into an ellipsoid rather than a sphere.
    This is the natural home for \textbf{H3/H4/H6's ``turbulence,''
    ``tears,'' ``cracks,'' and ``channels''}: a shear-dominated region
    stretches matter preferentially along one direction, which is
    exactly the qualitative picture of a filament or a crack, as
    opposed to $\theta$ (uniform swelling) or $\omega_{\mu\nu}$
    (rotation).
\end{itemize}
These three are not independent of curvature or of matter: they are
tied together by the \textbf{Raychaudhuri equation},
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} = -\frac{1}{3}\theta^2 -
\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu} + \omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu} -
R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu,
\end{equation}
where the last term is fixed, through the Einstein field equations,
by the local mass-energy density. This is the precise, already-
established sense in which ``fluidity'' cannot float free of a
mass-energy source (the open requirement raised against H2, H4, H5,
and H6 throughout \S\ref{sec:foundations} and \S\ref{sec:hypotheses}):
$\theta$, $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$, and $\omega_{\mu\nu}$ are properties of
how matter moves and curves spacetime, not properties spacetime can
be assigned independently of matter. \textbf{Using this formalism
does not rescue any hypothesis from that requirement} -- it hands you
the exact equation that requirement is written in, so that ``what
sources this'' becomes a specific term to compute rather than an open
question to wave at. Stated as plainly as possible: any hypothesis
that wants $\theta$ or $\omega_{\mu\nu}$ to \emph{cause} curvature,
rather than merely describe how a congruence of observers moves
through a spacetime whose curvature is already fixed by
$R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$, is proposing to change that last term --
which is an amendment to the Einstein field equations themselves, not
a free parameter available within them. H1b, H2, and H5 should each
be read as needing to say, explicitly, which term they are amending,
rather than leaving the kinematic triplet to imply a sourcing role
the Raychaudhuri equation does not, on its own, give it.

\paragraph{Inconsistency: the irregularity scalar $\mathcal{I}$.}
H3--H6 all invoke a third core term -- that space is not smooth, that
it is ``riddled with inconsistencies.'' This, too, already has a
rigorous counterpart to generalize rather than a word to leave
informal: standard cosmological \emph{perturbation theory} already
describes the real universe as a smooth background (homogeneous,
isotropic FLRW) plus deviations from it, and measures those
deviations with a dimensionless quantity (conventionally $\delta$,
the density contrast) relative to that background. Define, in the
same spirit, an \textbf{irregularity scalar}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{I}(x) \;:=\; \ell^2\sqrt{\,(\theta(x)-\bar\theta)^2 \;+\;
2\,\sigma_{\mu\nu}(x)\sigma^{\mu\nu}(x) \;+\; 2\,\omega_{\mu\nu}(x)
\omega^{\mu\nu}(x)\,}\,,
\end{equation}
where $\bar\theta$ is the smooth background expansion rate at that
cosmic time and $\ell$ is a reference \emph{time} scale fixed by
convention -- not a length, despite the suggestive symbol: $\theta$,
$\sigma_{\mu\nu}$, and $\omega_{\mu\nu}$ each carry units of inverse
time, so it is $\ell^2$ with units of $\mathrm{s^2}$, not
$\mathrm{m^2}$, that is needed to make $\mathcal{I}$ dimensionless
(\S\ref{sec:consistency}'s units check). $\mathcal{I}(x)=0$
recovers perfectly smooth FLRW space; $\mathcal{I}(x)>0$ is a
``spacetime inconsistency'' in H3--H6's sense, with its
\emph{magnitude} setting how large a departure it is and its
\emph{dominant term} ($\theta$, $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$, or
$\omega_{\mu\nu}$) setting which taxonomy species it looks like
(\S\ref{subsec:taxonomy}). This is deliberately \emph{not} how the
Sphere species (an actual black hole) should be defined, however: GR
already has an exact, independent, and far better-established
criterion for that -- a \textbf{trapped surface} (Penrose, 1965),
a closed surface from which even outward-directed light cannot
escape -- and the formation of a horizon should be checked against
that criterion, not against a large value of $\mathcal{I}$. Treating
$\mathcal{I}$ as graded, continuous ``inconsistency'' for the
Vortex/Depression/Crack species, while keeping the Sphere species
tied to the trapped-surface criterion it already has, is what keeps
the taxonomy from quietly promoting an ordinary density fluctuation
into a black hole merely because some scalar got large.

\textbf{What remains open:} the threshold value of $\mathcal{I}$ at
which a region counts as a named species at all (as opposed to an
ordinary, already-expected density fluctuation, which also has
$\mathcal{I}>0$ in this definition), and the reference scale $\ell$,
are not fixed by anything stated so far -- exactly like $\Sigma$'s
dependence and the Raychaudhuri source term, this is a free choice
the hypotheses need to make explicitly before $\mathcal{I}$ does
predictive work rather than descriptive work.

\paragraph{How this maps onto the taxonomy (\S\ref{subsec:taxonomy}).}
A natural first pass: \emph{Sphere} = a trapped surface is present,
regardless of $\mathcal{I}$; \emph{Vortex} = $\mathcal{I}>0$,
$\omega_{\mu\nu}$-dominated, no trapped surface; \emph{Depression} =
$\mathcal{I}>0$, small and localized, $\theta<0$-dominated, no
trapped surface; \emph{Crack/channel} = $\mathcal{I}>0$,
$\sigma_{\mu\nu}$-dominated, elongated, no trapped surface. This is
offered as a starting point for filling in the taxonomy table's open
columns, not as a settled result.

\subsection{Hypothesis 1: Gravity as Elasticity of Space}
\label{subsec:h1}
\textbf{Statement (scoped form).} Space can bend, and space has
elasticity. Gravity -- as a measurable quantity, a value of $g$, a
redshift, an orbit -- is calculable from the amount of bend, through
a fixed elastic constant of space. H1 takes no position on why
bending happens, or why mass responds to it; that causal question is
left entirely to H2. H1's claim is quantitative, not mechanistic: how
much bend, converted to how much gravity, via a constant that does
not vary from body to body.

\textbf{Defensible core (established; checked directly against five
bodies).} Three specific points survive direct numerical checking and
are adopted as this hypothesis's settled foundation:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{(1)}] \textbf{Elasticity is a universal constant, not
    a variable one.} Defining $\Sigma(x) := c^4/(8\pi G(x))$
    (\S\ref{subsec:core_terms}), the question of whether $\Sigma$
    varies from body to body reduces to the question of whether $G$
    does. It does not, to the precision of every available test: $G$
    is measured identically for Earth, the Sun, Mars, Jupiter, and
    Saturn (recovering their actual surface gravities via
    $g=\tfrac{4}{3}\pi G\rho R$ to the percent level once each body's
    own $\rho$ and $R$ are used), and independent bounds on
    $\dot G/G$ from lunar laser ranging and pulsar timing constrain
    any time variation to parts in $10^{11}$--$10^{12}$ per year.
    $\Sigma$ is adopted here as $\Sigma_0$, constant, on this
    evidence -- not as an unexamined assumption.
  \item[\textbf{(2)}] \textbf{The amount of bend is set by mass and
    distance from the body's center, not by density and radius taken
    as independent ingredients.} The more fundamental law, by
    Newton's shell theorem, is that the field at a point depends only
    on the enclosed mass $M$ and the distance $r$ from the center:
    $g(r) = GM/r^2$ outside any spherically symmetric body, regardless
    of density or internal structure. ``Density and radius'' is the
    special case of this law evaluated at a body's own surface
    ($r=R$), where $M = \rho\cdot\tfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3$ folds density
    and radius together; it is not a separate, additional dependence.
    Checked directly: a body with Earth's mass but twice Earth's
    radius (density $\approx689\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$) gives the same $g$
    at that radius as real Earth gives at the same distance from its
    own center -- confirming density itself carries no independent
    information once mass and distance are fixed.
  \item[\textbf{(3)}] \textbf{H1 computes gravity from bend; it does
    not assert that bend is gravity's cause.} This mirrors the
    Einstein field equations' own epistemic status: $G_{\mu\nu} =
    8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}/c^4$ is a correspondence between curvature and
    mass-energy, not a mechanistic derivation of why mass-energy
    curves anything -- GR has never answered that ``why'' either.
    Scoping H1 the same way removes a standing tension with H2: H1 is
    the quantitative layer (how much bend, how much gravity, and the
    fixed conversion constant between them); H2 is the mechanistic
    layer (why bending happens, why mass ``sinks''). They stack
    rather than compete for the same explanatory job.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{A taxonomy of bending, and gravity as a journey into slower
time.} This paper's own material gives exactly three distinct ways
something can bend space, worth stating as a set rather than leaving
scattered: (1) a mass simply sitting in space -- the passive,
source-centered case H1 itself describes, with no additional process
invoked, elasticity alone setting the amount; (2) a \emph{centripetal}
process -- something continuously sinking toward a center (H2a's
candidate driver) -- bending space further via the equivalence
principle's acceleration-as-field correspondence; (3) a
\emph{centrifugal} process -- something flung outward from an axis by
rotation (H2b's candidate driver) -- the only other
acceleration-based route that correspondence makes available. Items
(2) and (3) are H2's proposed causal layer, not H1's; H1 by itself
takes no position on which, if either, is at work.

Phrased in terms of the mechanism already derived in
\S\ref{subsec:h2} -- that the acceleration a falling body feels is
exactly $c^2$ times the spatial gradient of its own clock rate,
$a=-\partial_r\Phi$ -- gravity admits a vivid, concrete restatement:
\emph{falling is a journey toward where time passes more slowly}.
Relative to a distant clock, the falling body's clock increasingly
lags, falling further behind the longer the journey continues --
loosely, a journey ``back'' in time in the sense that the gap between
its own elapsed time and the distant clock's grows without bound.
This is a restatement of the $g_{00}$-gradient mechanism already
established, not a literal reversal of time's direction: nothing in
this journey runs backward, it only ever runs slower. It is also not
a new claim distinct from H8's own ``feature of time'' language
(\S\ref{subsec:h8}) -- it is the same mechanism, stated here at the
level of ordinary gravity rather than at the extreme, core-sinking
limit H8 describes.

\emph{The deepening of this lag with bend, checked numerically.} The
lag rate at radius $r$ is $\Phi(r)/c^2=-GM/(rc^2)$ -- ``how much bend''
in the time-lag sense -- and it deepens as $r$ decreases, exactly
tracking the acceleration, since $a=-\partial_r\Phi$ is the lag
rate's own spatial slope. For Earth: at the surface
($r=6.371\times10^6\,\mathrm{m}$), the lag rate is
$\approx-6.95\times10^{-10}$; at GPS orbit
($r=2.657\times10^7\,\mathrm{m}$), it is $\approx-1.67\times10^{-10}$
-- about four times shallower. Their difference, over one day,
reproduces the measured GPS correction directly:
\begin{equation}
(6.95-1.67)\times10^{-10}\times86{,}400\,\mathrm{s} \;\approx\; 4.56\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s} \;=\; 45.6\,\mu\mathrm{s/day},
\end{equation}
matching \S\ref{sec:consistency}.3's already-cited $45.7\,\mu$s/day
figure. Over the same two altitudes, $g$ itself falls from
$9.82\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at the surface to $0.565\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at GPS
orbit -- both the lag and the acceleration deepen together with the
bend, as they must, since one is literally the spatial slope of the
other.

\emph{A second layer worth naming: the gaps between successive steps
of this fall are not constant either.} $a=-\partial_r\Phi$ is itself a
function of $r$, not a fixed value, so as a body falls closer in, the
\emph{increments} of lag and speed it picks up per step of distance
fallen themselves grow -- a second derivative, not just a first:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d^2\Phi}{dr^2} = \frac{2GM}{r^3},
\end{equation}
which is exactly the tidal/curvature term already used in
\S\ref{sec:consistency}.1 and \S\ref{subsec:h2}'s discussion of true,
frame-independent curvature (for Earth, $\approx3.1\times10^{-6}
\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ per meter at the surface, $\approx3086$ Eötvös) --
the one quantity that stays nonzero in every frame, unlike a
centrifuge's merely apparent pseudo-gravity. This is worth flagging
as a genuinely open thread rather than a settled result: whether
gravity's full story is better told through this second-derivative,
widening-gap structure, rather than through the first-derivative
acceleration term alone, is not resolved here -- the identification
above only establishes that the familiar tidal term already in this
paper is the natural home for that intuition, not that it supplies a
new mechanism beyond what tidal curvature already does in standard
GR.

\textbf{A note on scope, restated for this point specifically.} None
of this is offered as a claim that GR is wrong. GR's agreement with
experiment -- Mercury's perihelion, light bending, binary-pulsar
orbital decay, direct gravitational-wave detections, the GPS figure
re-derived above -- is not in question. The claim worth taking
seriously is narrower: GR's two standing simplifying assumptions,
that spacetime is smooth and that empty space contributes nothing of
its own, are assumptions, not something GR itself proves, and this
paper's own working postulates (P1--P3) are an attempt to ask what, if
anything, changes if one or both are relaxed -- while requiring, as
H1 and H1b already do, that any such change vanish exactly in every
regime where GR has already been tested to the precision above.
\textbf{Formal statement.}
\begin{equation}
g(r) \;=\; \frac{GM}{r^2} \;=\; \frac{c^4}{8\pi\Sigma_0}\cdot\frac{M}{r^2},
\qquad \Sigma_0 = \frac{c^4}{8\pi G} \approx 4.98\times10^{42}\,\mathrm{N}
\ \text{(constant)}.
\end{equation}
Compare to the Einstein field equations, which already relate
curvature to mass-energy through this same fixed coupling:
\begin{equation}
G_{\mu\nu} \;=\; \frac{1}{\Sigma_0}\,T_{\mu\nu}.
\end{equation}
In this scoped form, H1's content is exactly the recovery of standard
Newtonian/GR surface gravity, with $\Sigma_0$ playing $G$'s role --
no more, no less, until a variation in $\Sigma$ is proposed (see H1b,
below).

\textbf{Motivation.} Gravity in general relativity already states
that curvature is produced by mass-energy, through the constant $G$.
H1 reinterprets that constant as a physical elasticity -- a
relabeling with a clear, checkable meaning, not yet an independent
discovery, because every number checked so far is the one standard
physics already predicts.

\textbf{What would falsify it / what is still undefined.}
Points (1)--(3) above are no longer open items: they have been
checked directly and incorporated as this hypothesis's settled core.
What remains open is whether H1 has anything to say \emph{beyond}
this recovery of standard gravity:
(i) As stated, with $\Sigma$ held constant, H1 makes no prediction
that differs from standard GR and is therefore not yet falsifiable in
its own right -- it becomes a hypothesis with independent content
only if $\Sigma$ is allowed to vary (with density, curvature, or the
irregularity scalar $\mathcal{I}$, \S\ref{subsec:core_terms}) in a way
the measured constant $G$ does not, and only in a regime not already
excluded by the $\dot G/G$ bounds in point (1). (ii) Any such
variation must still reduce to the constant $\Sigma_0$ everywhere $G$
has actually been measured. (iii) A genuinely novel extension of H1
(provisionally, H1b) would propose a specific, additional contribution
to bend tied to $\mathcal{I}(x)$ -- e.g.\ $g_{\text{total}} = GM/r^2 +
f(\mathcal{I})$ -- with a stated magnitude for $f$, checkable against
regions of high turbulence or inconsistency (H3--H6); this is flagged
as the open direction for future work, not yet developed here.

\subsection{Hypothesis 1b: Variable Elasticity Tied to Local Field Conditions}
\label{subsec:h1b}
\textbf{Statement (rough form).} H1's constant $\Sigma_0$ was checked
only against \emph{which body} is sourcing the field (Earth vs.\ the
Sun vs.\ Jupiter, etc.) and found not to vary that way. H1b asks a
different question: does $\Sigma$ depend on \emph{local field
conditions} -- how much space is already bent or disturbed right
where the field is being evaluated -- rather than on which body is
doing the bending? If so, the exact shape-indifference that follows
from constant $\Sigma$ (any mass distribution with the same total $M$
produces the same external field, \S\ref{subsec:h1} point 2) would no
longer hold exactly: a tightly concentrated mass and a spread-out mass
of the same $M$ could, in principle, leave measurably different
fields outside themselves, because the intervening space's own
elasticity would no longer be a neutral, fixed background.

\textbf{Formal statement.} Generalize $\Sigma$ from a constant to a
field that responds to the irregularity scalar $\mathcal{I}(x)$
already defined in \S\ref{subsec:core_terms}:
\begin{equation}
\Sigma(x) \;=\; \Sigma_0\Big[\,1 + \lambda\, h\big(\mathcal{I}(x)\big)\Big],
\qquad h(0) = 0,
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is a dimensionless coupling (sign and magnitude
unspecified) and $h$ is some function, also unspecified, vanishing
when space is locally undisturbed ($\mathcal{I}=0$, recovering
$\Sigma=\Sigma_0$ exactly). The field equation becomes
$G_{\mu\nu} = T_{\mu\nu}/\Sigma(x)$, now genuinely nonlinear, since
$\mathcal{I}(x)$ is itself built from the kinematic quantities
($\theta,\sigma_{\mu\nu},\omega_{\mu\nu}$) that the curvature equation
also governs.

\textbf{Motivation.} This is the only way, within this paper's own
formalism, to recover the shape-sensitive intuition the honey/ping-
pong-ball analogy was reaching for (\S\ref{subsec:h1} discussion):
mechanical media (honey) respond to an object's shape because they
interact through real contact forces; a field theory with constant
$\Sigma$ cannot, by the shell theorem, however counterintuitive that
is. Letting $\Sigma$ respond to local field conditions -- rather than
to which body is present -- is the one lever left that could restore
shape-sensitivity without contradicting anything already confirmed
about $\Sigma$ not differing between bodies.

\textbf{A motivating image: pseudo-centripetal coupling.} Picture a
lift (space) rising at a perfectly steady speed, with a vertical bar
(time) passing through it, fixed to the outside world. Letting the
bar slide loosely through your grip costs nothing; gripping it
tighter resists the slide, and that resistance is what presses you
into the floor -- the felt, outward, normal-force side of weight
(\S\ref{subsec:core_terms}'s distinction between felt and free-fall
acceleration), not the inward free-fall motion itself, hence
\emph{pseudo}-centripetal. The image's only job here is to motivate
why a coupling between a body and the local flow of time might vary
at all: a looser or tighter grip is a direct stand-in for a smaller or
larger $\lambda\,h(\mathcal{I}(x))$ in the formal statement above.

\emph{Where this agrees with established physics, and where it
doesn't.} It agrees, and is simply a restatement, wherever it
reduces to the $g_{00}$-gradient mechanism already derived and
numerically checked in \S\ref{subsec:h1}/\S\ref{subsec:h2} (the
GPS figure, the $9.82\to0.565\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ comparison): that
mechanism does not depend on this image at all, and holds regardless
of whether the image is accepted. Where it departs is the specific
claim this image is actually standing in for -- that the
\emph{strength} of that coupling ($\Sigma$, the ``grip'') is itself
variable rather than the fixed $\Sigma_0$ every existing test has
measured. That departure is not a restatement of anything in GR; it
is H1b's own open claim, tiered (c) exactly as stated above, and it
inherits every open problem already listed below -- most directly,
that $h$ and $\lambda$ are unspecified, and that wherever $\Sigma_0$
has already been confirmed constant (the solar system, laboratory
$G$, pulsar timing), this coupling must be shown to go to zero there,
not simply asserted to. The honest way to use this hypothesis, per
its own stated purpose, is as a question still being asked -- why a
body accelerates at all, one level beneath where GR's own
geodesic equation stops explaining and starts simply describing --
not as a settled answer competing with GR's tested results.

\textbf{Relation to existing programs.} This is not a new category of
idea -- it is close to two existing, well-developed, and tightly
constrained research programs. \textbf{Scalar-tensor gravity}
(Brans--Dicke, 1961, and successors) promotes $G$ itself to a
dynamical scalar field, $1/G \propto \phi(x)$, sourced by its own field
equation -- formally almost exactly H1b's $\Sigma(x)$, just coupled to
the trace of $T_{\mu\nu}$ directly rather than to $\mathcal{I}(x)$.
Solar-system tests already constrain this precisely: Cassini-spacecraft
Shapiro time-delay tracking (Bertotti, Iess \& Tortora, 2003) bounds
the Brans--Dicke parameter $\omega > 40{,}000$, meaning any
scalar-tensor-style deviation from constant $G$ in the solar system is
constrained to be extremely small -- a direct, numerical precedent for
how tightly H1b's $\lambda\,h(\mathcal{I})$ term is already bounded
wherever $\mathcal{I}$ is solar-system-sized. \textbf{MOND} (Milgrom,
1983; already discussed for H6, \S\ref{sec:foundations}.3) is the
nearer precedent for the \emph{style} of H1b's claim -- a response
that activates based on a local field condition rather than on source
identity -- though MOND's trigger is a universal acceleration scale
$a_0$, not $\mathcal{I}(x)$, and the two should not be treated as the
same proposal without showing they coincide.

\textbf{What would falsify it / open problems.}
(i) $h(\mathcal{I})$ is not yet specified; until it is, H1b makes no
prediction and is not yet falsifiable. (ii) Wherever $\Sigma$ has
already been confirmed constant -- the solar system, laboratory $G$
measurements, pulsar timing (\S\ref{subsec:h1} point 1) -- $\mathcal{I}$
is presumably small, but this needs to be checked explicitly: $h$ and
$\lambda$ must be small enough there that $\Sigma \approx \Sigma_0$ to
the precision already measured (at minimum, as tightly as the
Cassini $\omega>40{,}000$ bound already requires of comparable
theories). (iii) H1b therefore only has content if a regime exists
where $\mathcal{I}$ is large enough for $\lambda\,h(\mathcal{I})$ to
produce a checkable deviation -- candidates include near the compact,
high-curvature cores invoked by H3/H4/H8, or cosmic-web filaments
(H3/H4's taxonomy) -- and a specific prediction in one such regime,
not yet stated here, is the next concrete step. (iv) Distinguishing
H1b's $\mathcal{I}$-dependence from MOND's $a_0$-dependence, and from
plain scalar-tensor gravity's direct $T_{\mu\nu}$-coupling, is
necessary before H1b can claim to be a distinct proposal rather than
a relabeling of either. (v) \emph{Birkhoff's theorem is the specific
obstacle, and evading it should be named for what it is.} A static,
spherically symmetric vacuum exterior is forced to be exactly
Schwarzschild whenever the coupling is constant
(\S\ref{sec:consistency}.1). A $\Sigma$ that responds to $\mathcal{I}$
can only evade that conclusion by putting real stress-energy, or a
genuine additional field, into what GR would otherwise treat as empty
exterior space -- which is an amendment to the Einstein field
equations, not a parameter varying within them. H1b should state this
amendment explicitly, rather than let ``$\Sigma$ varies'' stand in for
a change of theory it has not yet written down as one.

\subsection{Hypothesis 2: Gravity as the Felt Consequence of Spacetime Curvature}
\label{subsec:h2}
\textbf{Statement (rough form).} Gravity is, at root, either a
centripetal process (something continuously sinking toward a center)
or a centrifugal one (something flung outward from an axis) -- and
the two make opposite, checkable predictions for the direction of
gravitational time dilation. A clock held near a mass runs slower
than a distant clock (confirmed daily by the GPS correction and
directly by optical-clock height comparisons): exactly the signature
of a centripetal process, where the body being accelerated is pulled
toward the ``floor'' of an accelerating frame, not a centrifugal one,
which would require the opposite sign (\S\ref{subsec:h2}'s thought
experiment below works through this in full). This favors a
centripetal origin for gravity over a centrifugal one before any of
the rest of this subsection's objections are even reached. (A
precision worth stating once: the acceleration an accelerometer
actually \emph{feels} standing on Earth's surface points outward --
the normal force balancing gravity -- while it is the
\emph{free-fall} acceleration of an unsupported body that is
centripetal, i.e. center-seeking. ``Centripetal'' here describes that
free-fall tendency, the thing H2 is trying to source, not the felt
support force.)

\textbf{Revision, superseding the paragraph above's original framing.}
This subsection originally proposed the reverse order -- bent space as
a \emph{consequence} of some other driver (cosmic expansion or spin),
with gravity itself caused by that driver rather than by the bend.
Having checked both candidate drivers against real numbers, this
paper now adopts the opposite, and simpler, order as its defining
statement: spacetime curvature, sourced by mass, is the cause; the
felt force called gravity is its consequence. (2a) is retired as a
\emph{driver} for exactly this reason -- there is no need for
cosmic expansion or any other external driver once curvature itself
is allowed to be the cause -- and is kept only as the label for the
centripetal, curvature-is-the-cause reading developed below. (2b)
is kept as the centrifugal candidate specifically because it lets
this paper show, concretely, what gravity is \emph{not}: a spin-based
felt force with the same sign as real gravity but zero true,
frame-independent curvature behind it (\S\ref{subsec:h2}'s discussion
of the tidal/Riemann distinction). By the equivalence principle, a
sustained felt acceleration of $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ (an elevator, a
rocket) is indistinguishable, locally, from a gravitational field --
but only the radial, curvature-sourced case (labeled ``centripetal''
here only as shorthand, not in the strict textbook sense; see the
formal statement and its note just below) is genuine gravity under
this paper's definition; the centrifugal case merely mimics it.

\textbf{Formal statement, current.} Gravity, under this paper's
defining statement, is
\begin{equation}
a^i \;=\; -\partial_i\Phi \;=\; -c^2\Gamma^i_{00},
\end{equation}
the Newtonian limit of the geodesic equation, built entirely from
$g_{00}\approx-(1+2\Phi/c^2)$ with no spatial-metric term entering at
this order (\S\ref{subsec:h2}'s H2c discussion below). This is the
equation this paper stands on for H2; the two boxed equations below
are retired and kept only as a record of the hypothesis's original,
superseded form.

\textbf{Formal statement, original (retired).}
% PLACEHOLDER, kept for the record only -- "sinking into space" needed
% a precise kinematic definition before this could be written as an
% equation, and neither candidate below survived the numeric check:
\begin{align*}
\text{(2a)-engine, withdrawn:} &\quad \vec a_{\text{gravity}} \;\overset{?}{=}\; \vec a_{\text{cosmic expansion}}(\text{mass, position}), \\
\text{(2b), not true gravity:} &\quad \vec a_{\text{gravity}} \;\overset{?}{=}\; \omega^2 r \,\hat\rho \ \text{(centrifugal form)}
\end{align*}

\textbf{(2a), revised: gravity as the felt consequence of spacetime
curvature itself.} (2a)'s original engine -- a literal, ongoing
acceleration of cosmic expansion, mapped onto gravity via the
equivalence principle -- is retired as stated, for the reason already
derived numerically below (objection (ii)): $cH_0\approx6\times10^{-10}
\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is roughly ten orders of magnitude short of
$9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and is the same everywhere rather than
centered on Earth. The problem is specifically this magnitude and
shape mismatch, not that a sustained cosmic acceleration is
implausible on its own terms (the universe's expansion genuinely is
accelerating, and under $\Lambda$CDM is expected to keep doing so
indefinitely) -- it is simply the wrong size and the wrong shape to
be Earth's gravity.

What (2a) keeps, having dropped that engine, is the conclusion
already established above: gravity is radial and mass-centered --
this paper's loose use of ``centripetal'' for that radial character,
not centrifugal, in character. (A terminological note, since the
word is used repeatedly below: strictly, ``centripetal'' names the
sideways acceleration, $v^2/r$, that holds a body at constant speed
on a circular path. The term this paper actually needs -- $-\nabla\Phi$,
radial, changing a body's speed from rest -- is not that. This paper
keeps using ``centripetal'' only as a loose label for ``radially
inward, toward a mass,'' and the reader should not read any of the
stricter, circular-motion content of the word into it.) What
produces that radial character is spacetime curvature itself, sourced
by mass, with no separate ingredient standing between them: each
point in space is connected, by the curvature there, to a local rate
of clock flow slightly slower than its neighbors' (not an earlier
\emph{date} or moment -- a lower clock ticks slower, it does not sit
in the past; rate and date are different things, and nothing in this
paper's mechanism picks out a preferred time direction); as the
curvature deepens, the gap between neighboring clock \emph{rates}
widens, and that widening gap \emph{is} the gravitational acceleration
($a=-\partial_r\Phi$, the Newtonian limit of the geodesic equation,
\S\ref{subsec:h1}). What the earlier draft of this section called a
``coupling'' is this same quantity under a different name, not an
additional thing curvature causes -- the lift/bar image below should
be read as a picture of curvature itself, not of a separate mechanism
sitting alongside it.

\textbf{Thought Experiment 1: the lift and the time bar.} A lift (space) rises at
a perfectly steady, non-accelerating pace. A vertical bar (time),
fixed to the world outside the lift, passes through a slot and
slides freely past as the lift rises. Each point along the bar marks
a different local clock \emph{rate}, not a different date -- farther
down the bar, clocks run slower, not earlier. Reaching out and taking
hold of the bar is connecting yourself to one such rate:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A \emph{loose} grip connects you to a rate only slightly
    slower than the lift's own; the gap is small, and the felt
    pressure into the floor is slight.
  \item A \emph{firmer} grip connects you to a slower rate still;
    the gap is larger, and the felt pressure is correspondingly
    greater.
  \item A \emph{fully locked} grip connects you to the rate at the
    point of deepest curvature, where the gap is largest and the
    pressure is maximal -- squashed to the floor.
\end{itemize}
How tightly you grip is not an independently chosen dial -- it is set
by how much curvature is present at your location, exactly as the
bend in H1 is set by mass and distance, not chosen freely. Note again
what never changes: the lift's own speed. It stays steady throughout;
nothing about the lift itself accelerates.

\emph{Summary.} The felt weight here is produced neither by the lift
accelerating (it doesn't) nor by true center-seeking motion as a
separate cause -- it is produced directly by the curvature's own
structure: the widening gap between successive local clock rates as
curvature deepens. This gap-widening is the single thing being
pictured, under two names -- ``grip'' in the lift image, $a=-\partial_r
\Phi$ in the formal statement, and curvature in the geometric
language of \S\ref{subsec:core_terms}. None of these three is prior
to, or causes, the others; they are the same fact.

\emph{Definition of gravity, revised.} Gravity is the felt
consequence of spacetime curvature: specifically, the widening gap
between the local clock rate at one point in space and the local
clock rate at a neighboring point, as the mass-sourced curvature
between them deepens. This is a restatement, not a new mechanism --
it is the Newtonian limit of the geodesic equation, and it is
consistent with the GPS correction, the Chou et al.\ clock data, and
the Earth tidal-gradient figure (\S\ref{subsec:h1}, \S\ref{subsec:h2}'s
discussion above) -- but those three are one potential, $\Phi=-GM/r$
for Earth, read three ways, not three independent confirmations of a
mechanism beyond GR. It pins the \emph{mechanism} down as real and
already well established, not illusory -- but it does not, by itself,
answer why mass curves spacetime's time axis this particular way in
the first place; that remains exactly as open as it is under plain GR
(\S\ref{subsec:h1}, point 3). H1b's
proposed variability of $\Sigma(x)$, if it means anything under this
reading, has to be understood as curvature having its own local
texture ($\mathcal{I}(x)$, already built from curvature-adjacent
kinematic quantities) rather than as a second, independently-sourced
coupling layered on top of curvature -- otherwise H1b would be
proposing an ingredient this section has just shown does not exist
separately from curvature itself.

\textbf{Motivation.} The equivalence principle is real and
well-tested: locally, uniform acceleration and a uniform gravitational
field are experimentally indistinguishable. H2 extrapolates this local
equivalence into a global claim about the \emph{origin} of every
gravitational field.

\textbf{What would falsify it / open problems.} Objections (i)--(v)
below all target \emph{H2-engine} -- the retired cosmic-expansion or
cosmic-spin driver described in the original Statement above -- not
the curvature-is-cause revision this paper now keeps (H2-revised,
\S\ref{subsec:h2}'s ``(2a), revised'' discussion); H2-revised is the
standard Newtonian limit of GR's geodesic equation and is not
challenged by any of (i)--(v), precisely because it no longer posits
any of the drivers these objections are about.
(i) Known gravitational fields (Earth's, the Sun's) are centered on
specific masses, static to high precision in the lab frame, and fall
off as $1/r^2$ from that mass; the hypothesis as stated does not yet
show how a universe-wide expansion rate or a universe-wide spin rate
produces a field with exactly that mass-centered, inverse-square
structure rather than a uniform or axis-centered one. (ii) The
measured acceleration of cosmic expansion (from dark energy) is many
orders of magnitude too small, and far too uniform/non-local, to
supply $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at Earth's surface without an additional,
yet-unstated amplification mechanism tied to local mass -- concretely,
the velocity-drift acceleration $cH_0\sim3\times10^8\times2.18\times10^{-18}
\approx6\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is roughly ten orders of
magnitude short of Earth's $g$, and the fractional rate $\ddot
a/a\sim H_0^2\sim4.75\times10^{-36}\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}$ is not itself an
acceleration (its units are $\mathrm{s^{-2}}$, not $\mathrm{m/s^2}$)
and so cannot be compared to $g$ directly; converting it to an
acceleration requires multiplying by a length scale, and the natural
one here is Earth's own radius: $H_0^2 R\approx4.75\times10^{-36}
\times6.371\times10^6\approx3\times10^{-29}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, roughly
\emph{thirty} orders of magnitude short of $g$, not ten. Either way
the shortfall is decisive, and neither quantity is centered on the
Earth to begin with. (iii) A
\emph{global} rotation of the universe -- a single, coherent spin
axis for the universe as a whole -- is tightly constrained by the
observed isotropy of the cosmic microwave background
(\S\ref{sec:foundations}.1) to be far too small to source
planetary-scale gravity. This bound is specifically about a global,
uniform rotation; it says nothing about an undefined \emph{local}
spin, so (2b) needs to say explicitly whether ``spin of space'' means
this tightly-bounded cosmic rotation or a different, as yet
undefined, local spin -- the two are not interchangeable, and only
the former currently has a precise observational bound attached. (iv) Both variants currently need the equivalence
principle to do more work than it is usually read as doing: it
licenses treating acceleration \emph{as} a gravitational field
locally, but does not by itself explain why mass generates an
inverse-square field centered on itself. (v) \emph{Uniform driver,
mass-proportional response.} The posited driver -- cosmic expansion
or cosmic spin -- is, by hypothesis, the same everywhere; it does not
vary from body to body. Yet the resulting bend is observed to scale
with mass ($g\propto M$). A uniform acceleration applied equally
everywhere does not, by itself, explain why a more massive body sinks
or bends space \emph{more} than a less massive one -- getting that
mass-proportional shape out of H2's story requires a second,
separate assumption about how mass modulates the response to the
driver, and that assumption is, structurally, H1's mass-coupling
($\Sigma$) again, reintroduced rather than derived from the
acceleration/spin mechanism itself. H2 therefore does not yet avoid
needing H1's coupling; it relocates where that coupling has to be
assumed, rather than explaining it away. Pressing this is also what
clarifies the alternative, non-circular reading available to H1 on
its own (\S\ref{subsec:h1}, point 3): that mass-space coupling may
simply be a brute, unexplained interaction -- exactly the epistemic
status the Einstein field equations themselves already have, with no
deeper derivation offered by GR, string theory, loop quantum gravity,
or any other current program either.

\textbf{Thought Experiment 2: distinguishing centripetal (2a) from
centrifugal (2b) by the sign of time dilation.} The equivalence principle makes a specific,
checkable prediction for each variant, and the two disagree with each
other, which gives a reader a concrete way to prefer one over the
other independent of \S\ref{sec:hypotheses}'s other objections.

\emph{Under (2a), constant acceleration (the elevator/rocket case):}
a clock near the ``floor'' -- the end of the elevator in the direction
of the acceleration, analogous to Earth's surface -- runs
\emph{slower} than a clock near the ``ceiling,'' exactly matching the
measured fact that clocks on Earth's surface run slower than clocks
farther from Earth (confirmed daily via the GPS correction, and
directly via optical-clock experiments over height differences of a
few centimeters). This is not a new derivation; it is the same
acceleration-based time dilation already used, uncontroversially, to
explain why the equivalence principle holds for clocks as well as for
felt force.

\emph{Under (2b), constant spin (the rotating-drum case):} the
relevant comparison is between a point near the rotation axis and a
point farther out, at the radius standing in for Earth's surface.
Here it matters precisely \emph{how} a body is supposed to be moving
with the spin. If a body is carried along \emph{with} the rotating
medium, with no motion relative to its own local surroundings (the
reading (2b) needs, since it treats the spin as a property of space
itself rather than of bodies moving through static space), no
established result in general relativity makes such a body's clock
run faster than distant, non-rotating space -- comoving observers in
an expanding universe and frame-dragged observers near a real
rotating mass both show the opposite pattern, or no net effect from
the carrying alone. If instead the body is read as kinematically
moving through otherwise-static space at the rotation rate (ordinary
special-relativistic motion, $v=\omega r$), that body's clock runs
\emph{slower} farther from the axis -- the right sign, but for a
reason that only applies to a body genuinely moving relative to
space, which is a different, narrower claim than (2b) as stated
(``spin of space'' itself, not bodies moving within static space).

\textbf{Conclusion a reader can draw:} (2a) reproduces the measured
sign of Earth's gravitational time dilation directly and without
qualification. (2b) reproduces it only by quietly switching to the
kinematic, bodies-moving-through-space reading, which is not the
claim (2b) was making; under the reading (2b) actually needs --
space itself carrying bodies along -- no established mechanism
supplies the correct sign at all. This gives a concrete reason to
prefer (2a) over (2b) specifically, independent of (and in addition
to) objections (i)--(iv) above. It does not rescue (2a) from those
other objections -- the magnitude of cosmic expansion's acceleration
and the mass-centered shape problem in (i)--(ii) are untouched by
this result and remain fully open.

\textbf{H2c (bend is geometry, not force).} The spatial curvature H1
computes redirects a moving body's \emph{direction} without changing
its \emph{speed}, and does so independent of how fast the body is
moving. This is a real effect, not an invented one -- it is exactly
the velocity-independent term that supplies half of gravitational
light-bending ($\delta_{\text{spatial}}=2GM/bc^2$, against a total
$\delta=4GM/bc^2$ for light). But it is not, by itself, an
accelerating force, and should not be mistaken for the whole of
gravity.

\textbf{Formal statement.} For a body of speed $v$ passing a mass $M$
at impact parameter $b$, the total small-angle deflection splits as
\begin{equation}
\delta \;\approx\; \underbrace{\frac{2GM}{bv^2}}_{\text{Newtonian ($g_{00}$) term}} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{2GM}{bc^2}}_{\text{spatial-curvature term, independent of }v},
\end{equation}
with ratio $\delta_{\text{spatial}}/\delta_{\text{Newton}}=v^2/c^2$.
The first term is the ordinary Newtonian deflection from $-\nabla\Phi$
(equivalently, from $g_{00}$); it is \emph{not} centripetal in the
strict sense -- it is radial and changes the body's speed, where
centripetal acceleration proper is the sideways, speed-preserving
term that holds a body on a circular path. This paper labels it
``centripetal'' elsewhere only as shorthand for ``radially inward,''
and that shorthand is dropped here, in the one equation in this
subsection, to keep the two senses from being conflated.

\textbf{Numeric check against the measured solar deflection.} The
total $\delta=4GM/(bc^2)$ is the standard GR light-bending formula,
transcribed here, not independently derived from $\Sigma$ or from
this paper's own machinery; evaluating it is a check that this
paper's bookkeeping reproduces a known number, not a new prediction.
For light grazing the Sun's limb, $GM_\odot\approx1.327\times10^{20}
\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$ and $b\approx R_\odot\approx6.96\times10^8\,
\mathrm{m}$, giving $\delta=4GM_\odot/(bc^2)\approx8.47\times10^{-6}
\,\mathrm{rad}\approx1.75''$ -- matching the historically measured
solar deflection (Eddington et al., 1919, and all subsequent
measurements) to the precision quoted. This specific number was not
checked in \S\ref{sec:consistency} before this revision, which still
correctly lists Mercury's perihelion precession and
gravitational-wave speed/waveform constraints as not yet worked
through numerically; light bending is removed from that list here,
having now been evaluated.

\textbf{Motivation.} \S\ref{subsec:h1}'s own defensible-core point 3
already notes that H1 computes gravity \emph{from} the bend without
asserting the bend \emph{causes} gravity -- a disclaimer, not a proof.
H2c supplies the proof: it names the specific sense in which bend
alone is insufficient, and ties the missing piece directly to H2a's
centripetal/time-dilation mechanism rather than leaving it
unaddressed.

\textbf{What would falsify it / open problems.}
(i) \emph{A body released from rest has nothing to redirect -- and the
named mechanism is the resolution, not a gap.} Bend-as-pure-redirection
changes the direction of an existing velocity; it has no mechanism for
a body with $v=0$ to begin moving at all. The resolution is that no
body is ever actually at rest in 4D spacetime: every object's
4-velocity has fixed magnitude $c$, so a body ``at rest'' in space is
moving entirely along the time axis. In the weak-field, slow-motion
limit, the geodesic equation gives
\begin{equation}
a^i \;=\; -c^2\,\Gamma^i_{00} \;=\; -\frac{c^2}{2}\,\partial_i g_{00} \;=\; -\partial_i\Phi,
\end{equation}
with $g_{00}\approx-(1+2\Phi/c^2)$ -- i.e. the spatial gradient of the
local clock rate, not the spatial-curvature term this hypothesis
isolates, is exactly what bends that time-axis motion into spatial
motion, starting from $v=0$. This is the standard Newtonian-limit
derivation of GR's geodesic equation, not a new postulate; it is cited
here because it is precisely the mechanism objection (i) shows is
missing from bend-as-pure-redirection alone, and it names H2a's
centripetal/time-dilation term concretely rather than leaving it as
an unspecified placeholder. Every observed instance of a falling
object -- starting at rest and gaining speed -- is this $g_{00}$-driven
acceleration from zero, which the spatial-bend mechanism alone cannot
produce. \emph{Checked numerically, against Earth:} the clock-rate
field $\Phi(r)/c^2=-GM/(rc^2)$ deepens from $\approx-1.67\times10^{-10}$
at GPS orbit ($r=2.657\times10^7\,\mathrm{m}$) to
$\approx-6.95\times10^{-10}$ at the surface
($r=6.371\times10^6\,\mathrm{m}$); the difference, accumulated over a
day, is $\approx45.6\,\mu$s -- matching the measured GPS correction
(\S\ref{sec:consistency}.3) directly. Over the same interval $g$ rises
from $0.565$ to $9.82\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$: the acceleration is exactly
this field's own spatial slope, so the deepening clock lag and the
growing acceleration are the same fact, not two correlated ones. (ii) \emph{Even for a moving body, the effect is too small by
seventeen orders of magnitude at ordinary speeds.} For $v=2\,\mathrm{m/s}$,
$v^2/c^2=(2)^2/(3\times10^8)^2\approx4.4\times10^{-17}$: if gravity
were only the velocity-independent spatial-bend term, a slow-moving
body would be deflected about $10^{17}$ times more weakly than
measured gravity actually deflects it. (iii) The spatial-curvature
term only becomes comparable to the centripetal term as $v\to c$,
which is precisely why it is observable for light (gravitational
lensing) and unobservable for everyday motion -- consistent with, and
not a competitor to, H2a's claim that the accelerating mechanism is
the time-dilation/centripetal piece, not the spatial bend. (iv) This
does not retire H1 -- H1's own statement was already scoped to avoid
this exact claim (\S\ref{subsec:h1}, point 3) -- it closes the loop on
\emph{why} that scoping was necessary, and gives H2a's centripetal
framing a concrete, numeric reason to be preferred over treating bend
itself as the source of force.

\textbf{Statement (rough form).} Space is not smooth; it is riddled
with inconsistencies in its fabric. Black holes are one type of such
inconsistency. Stellar-core-collapse black holes cannot grow very
massive. The largest black holes instead arise directly from large
tears or gaps in space, produced by uneven cosmic expansion and
turbulence.

\textbf{Formal statement.}

% claim in GR and needs to be defined before an equation is possible.
[PLACEHOLDER: define ``inconsistency'' as a geometric object --
a curvature singularity, a topological defect, a region where the

\textbf{Motivation.} Correctly notes a real, open astrophysical
puzzle: stellar-collapse black holes are bounded in mass (by
progenitor mass and pair-instability effects, roughly tens to a few
hundred solar masses), yet supermassive black holes
($10^6$--$10^{10}\,M_\odot$) are observed already in place within the
first billion years after the Big Bang -- the ``seed problem'' for
supermassive black holes, a genuinely unsolved question in current
astrophysics.

\textbf{What would falsify it / open problems.}
(i) In standard GR, spacetime is a smooth (differentiable) manifold
by construction; a literal ``tear'' is a topology change, which is
classically forbidden and, even in candidate quantum-gravity
treatments, is a highly specialized, unresolved research question --
not an established mechanism this hypothesis can yet borrow. (ii) The
nearest legitimate existing idea -- that spacetime is not perfectly
smooth at the Planck scale (``quantum foam,'' Wheeler 1955) -- operates
at $\sim10^{-35}\,\mathrm{m}$, some 30+ orders of magnitude below
anything that could seed a supermassive black hole; no established
theory currently bridges that scale gap, so it is not yet available as
support. (iii) Mainstream astrophysics already has several candidate
answers to the seed problem this hypothesis is competing with --
direct-collapse black holes from massive gas clouds, heavy seeds from
dense early star clusters, and super-Eddington accretion -- and H3
needs to say what it explains that these do not, or what observation
would distinguish ``a tear in space'' from a direct-collapse seed.

\subsection{Hypothesis 4: Widespread Small Inconsistencies as the Seed of All Accretion}
\label{subsec:h4}
\textbf{Statement (rough form).} Inconsistencies in space are common
and widespread, not just the rare large ones behind H3. These smaller
inconsistencies are small tears or holes that trap particles, seeding
the accretion of matter into stars, planets, and asteroids. Accretion
itself then bends space further, accelerating further collection.
It would not be wrong to say a black hole lies at the center of every
body in space.

\textbf{Formal statement.}
[PLACEHOLDER: as with H3, ``trap'' and ``hole'' need a precise
geometric or field-theoretic definition before this is an equation
rather than a picture]

\textbf{Motivation.} Correctly identifies that accretion is how stars,
planets, and asteroids form, and that growing mass deepens a
gravitational well, which is consistent with standard accretion
theory (gravitational instability and core-accretion models for star
and planet formation). The closest existing idea to ``a small hole
seeding accretion'' is the primordial black hole (PBH) hypothesis
(Zel'dovich \& Novikov, 1966; Hawking, 1971), in which density
fluctuations in the very early universe collapse directly into small
black holes, which have since been proposed as possible seeds for
structure formation and as a dark-matter candidate.

\textbf{What would falsify it / open problems.}
(i) Standard star and planet formation is already well modeled by
gravitational self-collapse of gas and dust under ordinary Newtonian/
GR gravity, with no need for an additional ``trapping'' mechanism; H4
needs to say what observation it predicts that the standard
core-accretion picture does not. (ii) The claim that \emph{every} body
in space -- every planet, asteroid, and ordinary star -- has a black
hole at its center is directly checked against existing evidence and,
as stated, is in tension with it: the Sun's interior structure is
measured via helioseismology, Earth's via seismology and its geodetic
and magnetic-field behavior (which requires an ordinary convecting
liquid-metal core), and none show the signatures a central black hole
would produce (anomalous mass-radius relation, an accretion luminosity
or steady mass growth, or the tidal/precision-tracking signatures a
compact central mass would leave). This is the most directly
falsifiable claim among all four hypotheses, and currently the one
with the most existing evidence against it as stated; narrowing it --
e.g., to large bodies only, or to the very early universe before
ordinary structure formed, closer to how the actual PBH hypothesis is
framed -- would bring it back into contact with open questions rather
than settled observations.

\subsection{Hypothesis 5: Supermassive Black Holes as Vortices from Colliding, Oppositely-Expanding Regions of Space}
\label{subsec:h5}
\textbf{Statement (rough form).} Ordinary (stellar-scale) black holes
are minor turbulence, tears, or inconsistencies in space. Supermassive
black holes are categorically different: they form where large
regions of space, expanding in opposing directions, collide and
create a large vortex. Supermassive black holes are not, in general,
of stellar-collapse origin -- though that origin is not impossible in
principle -- and share more in common with cyclones and hurricanes
than with collapsed stars.

\textbf{Formal statement.}
[PLACEHOLDER: ``regions of space expanding in opposing directions''
and ``collision'' need to be defined relative to the standard
cosmological picture, in which the expansion rate (the Hubble
parameter) is a single, roughly uniform, non-directional quantity at
a given cosmic time -- not a vector flow field with distinct, opposing
currents]

\textbf{Motivation.} Correctly inherits the real seed problem from H3:
a single star's collapse cannot directly produce a $10^6$--$10^{10}
\,M_\odot$ object, so some categorically different formation channel
for the largest black holes is a reasonable thing to look for. The
vortex picture also has a genuine, if indirect, point of contact with
real physics: real (rotating, Kerr) black holes drag spacetime around
them (frame dragging / the Lense--Thirring effect, now measured via
accretion-disk precession and X-ray spectroscopy), and this rotational
behavior is precisely what motivates \emph{analogue-gravity}
experiments, in which a real draining-fluid vortex is built in the lab
because the mathematics of waves near its drain matches the
mathematics of waves near a rotating black hole's horizon (Unruh,
1981; demonstrated with real draining-bathtub vortices showing
rotational superradiance). That match is at the level of the wave
equations, however, not a claim that spacetime itself is a literal
fluid.

\textbf{What would falsify it / open problems.}
(i) The standard cosmological model (the homogeneous, isotropic FLRW
spacetime, supported by the measured isotropy of the cosmic microwave
background and of large-scale structure) describes expansion as a
single scale factor increasing over time, not as a flow field with
distinct regions expanding in different directions that could
collide; H5 needs to state what observation would reveal such
oppositely-expanding regions, since none has been reported.
\emph{This specifically rules out a large-scale, coherently
directional version of colliding expansion} -- the CMB-isotropy bound
behind it constrains a single, global flow pattern
(\S\ref{sec:foundations}.1), not scattered, local patchiness. A
version of H5 built instead on small, randomly-distributed regions
with locally differing expansion histories is not excluded by this
bound; it instead has to clear the two items immediately below
(stress-energy content, and GR's smoothness assumption) and show it
is doing something the already-measured $10^{-5}$ density
perturbations do not already cover. (ii) Even
granting a collision, converting the kinetic energy of ``colliding
space'' into spacetime curvature strong enough to form a horizon
requires an effective stress-energy content for the colliding regions
--- the same open requirement flagged for H1 and H4, since GR curves
in response to stress-energy, not motion of space considered as
already-empty geometry. (iii) The nearest existing, if still
speculative, research precedent for ``collision forms a black hole''
is bubble-collision black-hole formation during an early-universe
phase transition, studied in specific inflationary/phase-transition
models -- but that mechanism is tied to the very early universe and a
specific particle-physics scenario, not to expansion generally or to
an ongoing, occasional process. (iv) Rotation (frame dragging) in
real supermassive black holes is well explained as inherited angular
momentum from the matter and smaller black holes that merged and
accreted to form them -- the standard growth picture H5 sets out to
replace -- so H5 would need to show what a vortex-of-colliding-space
origin predicts that spin-from-accretion-and-mergers does not.
(v) \emph{The Bullet Cluster test, run against this specific
mechanism.} If colliding space behaved like an ordinary, dissipative
fluid -- shocking and slowing on contact, the way the Bullet
Cluster's hot gas does -- a vortex sourced by it should track the gas,
not the galaxies; the Bullet Cluster shows the lensing mass tracking
the (collisionless) galaxies instead, which would rule this reading
out directly. H5 survives this test only by committing explicitly to
colliding space passing through itself \emph{nearly without
friction} -- the same near-collisionless behavior already assumed of,
and separately constrained for, particle dark matter. This is a real,
available option, not automatically excluded, but it is a specific
commitment H5 needs to state, not an automatic property of ``space
colliding'' -- a real medium usually has some self-interaction, and
asserting otherwise only to match this one observation is circular
unless an independent reason for near-frictionlessness is given.
(vi) \emph{Mass-energy content, and where it would have to come from.}
Point (ii) above already notes that curving spacetime requires a
stress-energy source, not just the relative motion of already-empty
geometry; stated plainly, this means H5 currently has no quantified
energy content for ``colliding space'' to supply. This is exactly the
gap \textbf{H7} (\S\ref{subsec:h7}) is attempting to fill from the
other direction -- if H7's claim that expansion/compression of space
carries real, quantifiable energy were established, it would hand H5
precisely the missing ingredient. As things stand, neither hypothesis
has independently delivered this number: H5 needs energy content
from moving space that H7 has not yet quantified, and H7's own
magnitude check (\S\ref{subsec:h7} discussion) found the effect
negligible at the present cosmic epoch for exactly this reason. H5 and
H7 should be read as leaning on the same unresolved piece, not as two
separately-supported hypotheses.

\subsection{Hypothesis 6: Galaxies as Matter Settled into a Vortex's Depression, Replacing Dark Matter}
\label{subsec:h6}
\textbf{Statement (rough form).} A galaxy is matter tracing out the
depression in space caused by a central vortex (a supermassive black
hole, per H5). Matter settles into this depression. Stars orbit the
central vortex at speeds set by the strength and reach of the
depression it causes; faster-than-expected orbital speeds at a
galaxy's edge come from the vortex's own strength and nature, and
dark matter is not required to explain them. Different galaxy
morphologies reflect different forms or strengths of the underlying
spacetime turbulence.

\textbf{Formal statement.}
[PLACEHOLDER: needs an explicit $v(r)$ (orbital speed as a function of
radius) predicted from ``vortex strength,'' to compare against the
measured rotation curves this hypothesis is meant to explain]

\textbf{Motivation.} Correctly identifies the real observation: galaxy
rotation curves are measured to stay flat (roughly constant orbital
speed) out to tens of kiloparsecs, far beyond where the visible
(stellar and gas) mass alone, via ordinary $1/r^2$ gravity, predicts a
declining (Keplerian) curve -- this is the original dark-matter
evidence (Rubin \& Ford, 1970s) and a real target for any competing
explanation.

\textbf{What would falsify it / open problems.}
(i) \emph{Scale mismatch.} A supermassive black hole's own
gravitational reach (its ``sphere of influence'') extends only a few
parsecs to a few tens of parsecs from the galactic center -- it
dominates stellar orbits only there. Flat rotation curves are
measured at tens of \emph{kilo}parsecs, roughly three to four orders
of magnitude farther out, where the central black hole's own
gravitational pull, by standard $1/r^2$ falloff, is already
negligible. For H6 to work, ``the vortex'' has to mean something with
far greater mass and reach than the black hole itself -- at which
point it is doing the explanatory work dark matter currently does,
under a new name, and needs its own mass profile stated and checked
against the data the way a dark-matter halo profile is. (ii) \emph{
Independent evidence beyond rotation curves.} Dark matter is inferred
through several lines of evidence besides rotation curves, each of
which a replacement needs to address: the Bullet Cluster, where
gravitational lensing maps show the dominant mass concentration
spatially separated from the hot, X-ray-emitting gas that holds most
of the ordinary matter in the colliding clusters -- direct evidence
for a gravitating component not tied to where the normal matter sits;
the relative heights of the cosmic microwave background's acoustic
peaks, which fix the ratio of ordinary to total matter in the very
early universe, long before galaxies or their central black holes
existed; and the growth rate of large-scale structure, which needs
the extra gravitational pull of a non-baryonic component to form
galaxies within the age of the universe at all. A vortex tied to a
galaxy's own central black hole has no obvious bearing on any of
these three, since two of them predate galaxy formation and one
(Bullet Cluster) is at cluster, not single-galaxy, scale. (iii) H6's
closest existing competitor is Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND;
Milgrom, 1983), which changes the force law itself at low
accelerations and reproduces many individual galaxies' rotation
curves strikingly well without extra matter -- but is also known to
struggle with exactly the cluster-scale and CMB evidence in (ii). If
H6 is a vortex-flavored version of the same idea, it inherits MOND's
real successes but should also expect, and address, MOND's known
difficulties rather than treating rotation curves as the only test.
(iv) \emph{Galaxy-to-galaxy diversity.} Galaxies of similar visible
(and presumably similar central-black-hole) mass are observed to
need very different amounts of inferred dark matter -- some
ultra-diffuse galaxies need a large extra-mass fraction, while a few
reported galaxies appear to need very little. A mechanism tied
tightly to a single central vortex's mass needs to explain this
spread; the standard dark-matter picture explains it as galaxies
having assembled with different amounts of dark-matter halo relative
to their visible mass, through different formation histories.

in this paper, because the general phenomenon it describes is real
and already established in three separate, serious contexts:
cosmological particle creation (Parker, 1968), in which the
expansion of spacetime stretches quantum vacuum fluctuations into
real particles -- the accepted mechanism by which inflation seeded
the density variations later seen in the cosmic microwave background;
reheating at the end of inflation, where the energy driving that
expansion converts directly into the real particles that went on to
form everything in the universe; the dynamical Casimir effect, in
which rapidly changing the boundary of a vacuum region has been
experimentally shown to create real photons (Wilson et al., 2011);
and Hawking radiation, in which the changing, compressing geometry
around a collapsing mass converts vacuum fluctuations into real,
radiated particles (Hawking, 1974). H7 is, in effect, the claim that
these four known mechanisms are instances of one general principle,
tying particle/energy creation to $\theta$ (or its rate of change)
rather than requiring each to be re-derived separately.

\textbf{What would falsify it / open problems.}
(i) \emph{Magnitude, for the present epoch.} Cosmological particle
creation becomes significant only when the expansion rate is
comparable to the particle's own natural (Compton) frequency,
$mc^2/\hbar$. Today's Hubble rate is approximately
$2\times10^{-18}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$; even the lightest known particle,
a neutrino of mass $\sim0.1\,\mathrm{eV}$, has a Compton frequency of
order $10^{14}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ -- a gap of roughly 32 orders of
magnitude. The one episode when this gap closed was inflation, not
the present day, so H7's effect, however real in principle, is not
occurring at any detectable rate in the universe as it expands right
now. (ii) \emph{Net extraction and energy conservation.} In every
established instance -- the dynamical Casimir effect, Hawking
radiation, inflationary reheating -- the vacuum acts as a conversion
mechanism, not a net energy source: driving the dynamical Casimir
effect costs energy to move the boundary, at least as much as is
recovered as photons; Hawking radiation draws down the radiating
black hole's own mass as its price; reheating converts the energy of
a specific, already-present field (the inflaton), it does not create
energy from nothing. ``This energy should be capturable'' is, in its
strongest reading, a claim that energy conservation can be
circumvented, which is a substantially harder barrier to clear than
any other objection raised in this paper -- $\dot G/G$ bounds, CMB
isotropy, or interior-structure data can all, in principle, be
satisfied by a sufficiently small or well-hidden effect; conservation
of energy is not a bound to be satisfied by smallness, but a
constraint on the bookkeeping itself. H7 is best read, for now, as
the unification claim in its \emph{motivation} (one principle behind
four known phenomena) without the extraction claim, unless and until
a specific mechanism is proposed that identifies exactly what pays
the energy's cost, the way the black hole's own mass pays for Hawking
radiation.

\subsection{Hypothesis 8: Black Hole Formation as a Core Sinking Into Time}
\label{subsec:h8}
\textbf{Statement (rough form).} When a collapsing star's core becomes
too massive for anything to support it against its own gravity, it
does not collapse to a literal point instantly; it becomes a dense,
finite-sized core that sinks into space gradually, the way a ball
bearing sinks slowly through honey. As it sinks, it becomes steadily
more a feature of the time dimension than of space, generating a
vortex as it goes. The point where the core finally disappears,
having sunk completely, is the singularity -- not a pre-existing
infinitesimal point the core falls into, but the gentle closing of
the gap behind it.

\textbf{Formal statement.}
[PLACEHOLDER: needs the core's radius as an explicit function of
proper time during collapse, $R_{\mathrm{core}}(\tau)$, and a
statement of what, physically, sets its minimum (non-zero) size]

\textbf{Motivation.} More of this maps onto real, already-established
or already-seriously-studied general relativity than any other
hypothesis in this paper:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{``Too heavy for anything to support it''} is exactly
    the real physics of collapse: electron degeneracy pressure
    supports a white dwarf only up to the Chandrasekhar limit
    ($\sim1.4\,M_\odot$); neutron degeneracy pressure supports a
    neutron star only up to the Tolman--Oppenheimer--Volkoff limit
    ($\sim2$--$3\,M_\odot$); beyond either limit, no known form of
    pressure halts the collapse.
  \item \emph{``Sinking into space, like a ball bearing in honey''}
    is a good intuitive restatement of the \emph{river model of
    gravity} already introduced for H2 (Painlev\'e, 1921;
    Gullstrand, 1922): in that already-standard, already-equivalent-
    to-Schwarzschild picture, space itself is pictured as flowing
    inward toward the mass, reaching the speed of light exactly at
    the horizon -- matter does not so much fall \emph{through} space
    as get carried inward \emph{by} it, very much like a bearing
    settling through a viscous fluid.
  \item \emph{``Becomes more a feature of the time dimension''} is not
    a loose metaphor -- it is a precise, well-known, and somewhat
    startling feature of the Schwarzschild solution itself: inside
    the horizon, the roles of the radial and time coordinates swap,
    so that moving toward the center becomes as unavoidable, and as
    direction-less, as moving forward in time is outside it. ``Falling
    toward the center is like being swept forward in time'' is a
    standard way this fact is described in general relativity
    courses, not a new claim.
  \item \emph{``A dense core, not an infinitely small point''} tracks
    a genuinely open and actively studied question: whether a true,
    infinite-density singularity actually forms (as classical GR's
    singularity theorems, Penrose and Hawking, predict under very
    general conditions) or whether quantum-gravitational effects halt
    collapse at some very small but finite size, replacing the
    singularity with something else -- proposals include ``Planck
    stars'' (Rovelli \& Vidotto, 2014) and ``fuzzballs'' (Mathur,
    2005, in string theory). H8's finite core is a plain-language
    version of this exact open question, not a departure from it.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What would falsify it / open problems.}
(i) \emph{The vortex needs its own, separate ingredient.} The river
model's inflow for a non-rotating collapse is purely radial -- no
swirling at all. A genuine vortex (frame dragging, the Kerr metric's
rotation) requires the collapsing core to carry angular momentum,
inherited from the progenitor star's own spin; it is not an automatic
consequence of ``sinking'' by itself. H8 needs to state explicitly
that the vortex component depends on, and scales with, the
progenitor's rotation -- without that, a non-rotating collapse in
this picture should produce no vortex at all, which is worth checking
against whichever of H3/H5's claims this is meant to connect to.
(ii) \emph{The finite-core size is not yet a specific claim.} Planck
stars and fuzzballs each make a specific proposal for what halts
collapse and at what scale (roughly the Planck length, or a horizon-
scale structure, respectively); H8 needs its own specific answer to
the same question -- what physically sets $R_{\mathrm{core}}$'s
minimum -- before it can be checked against, or distinguished from,
those two existing proposals.

\paragraph{A bounded extension: time's direction tipping, not just
slowing, inside the vortex.} H8 already uses one precise, established
fact about the Schwarzschild interior -- that the radial and time
coordinates swap roles, so infall becomes as unavoidable as moving
forward in time. Real \emph{rotating} spacetimes go further than this
in a way worth adding explicitly, rather than leaving as background
intuition: inside a Kerr black hole's ergosphere, and throughout
G\"odel's rotating-universe solution (1949, already cited for H2b),
light cones genuinely \emph{tip} -- the locally-available ``forward in
time'' direction reorients relative to a distant observer's, to the
point that G\"odel's solution permits closed timelike curves outright.
This is real, if exotic, general relativity, not speculation reaching
beyond it. Proposed addition, scoped narrowly: wherever H8's sinking
core carries genuine angular momentum (the still-open ingredient from
point (i) above), the resulting vortex should exhibit this same
light-cone tipping, not merely ordinary time dilation -- giving H8's
``more a feature of time than of space'' language a sharper, rotation-
specific reading it does not yet have. This is explicitly \emph{not}
a claim that time tips everywhere, or in any region without real
rotation; it is bounded to the same vortex regions H5 and H8 already
propose, and inherits H8's own open item (i) as a precondition: no
stated angular momentum means no tipping, exactly as it currently
means no vortex.

\subsection{Hypothesis 9: Gravity's Weakness as Leakage into a Hidden Dimension}
\label{subsec:h9}
\textbf{Statement (rough form).} Gravity is not intrinsically
different in kind from the other three forces -- it is diluted. Space
has at least one dimension beyond the four (three space, one time)
this paper has otherwise assumed throughout, and gravity alone is free
to spread into it, while matter and the other forces remain confined
to our own four. What we measure as gravity's strength -- including
the strain of a passing gravitational wave -- is a diluted remnant of
its true strength in that larger setting, not gravity's actual,
undiminished magnitude.

\textbf{Formal statement.}
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_0 \;\overset{?}{\sim}\; \Sigma_{\text{bulk}}\cdot
\left(\frac{L}{\ell_\ast}\right)^{n},
\end{equation}
where $\Sigma_{\text{bulk}}$ is gravity's coupling in the full
$(4+n)$-dimensional bulk, $L$ is the extra dimension(s)' size,
$\ell_\ast$ a fundamental length scale, and $n$ the number of extra
dimensions -- schematic, modeled directly on the ADD relation
$M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2 \sim M_\ast^{\,n+2}L^n$, not yet derived within
this paper's own formalism. The measured $\Sigma_0$
(\S\ref{subsec:core_terms}) is large -- gravity weak -- because
$L\gg\ell_\ast$, not because gravity's bulk coupling differs in kind
from the other forces'.

\textbf{Motivation.} The hierarchy problem is real and unsolved:
gravity is measured to be roughly $10^{36}$ times weaker than
electromagnetism between two protons, with no accepted explanation
for why gravity specifically is so feeble. H9 adopts an existing,
actively studied answer to this (\S\ref{sec:foundations}.3) rather
than proposing a new one, and connects it to this paper's own
language: $\Sigma_0$'s measured, constant value
(\S\ref{subsec:h1}, point 1) is reframed as a 4D-effective, diluted
projection of a deeper bulk constant, not a contradiction of H1's
result but an additional layer underneath it.

\textbf{What would falsify it / open problems.}
(i) $n$ and $\ell_\ast$ are not yet specified; until they are, H9
makes no numerical prediction. (ii) It is already tightly bounded
regardless: sub-millimeter torsion-balance tests of Newton's law
already exclude any such dimension larger than roughly
$40$--$50\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, and collider searches for missing-energy
gravitons constrain it further -- so the more dramatic reading this
hypothesis was originally reaching for (gravitational waves ``huge''
at any accessible scale) is specifically excluded; only a very small,
already-tightly-bounded version remains available. (iii) H9 must
still recover $\Sigma_0$'s measured constancy in every regime already
checked (\S\ref{subsec:h1}, point 1; \S\ref{sec:consistency}) --
dilution must be uniform enough, or $L$ stable enough, that no
detectable variation in $\Sigma_0$ results. (iv) H9 is, at present, an
adopted explanation, not a new one; its distinct content, if any,
would need to show what this paper's own formalism ($\Sigma$,
$\mathcal{I}$) adds to the existing ADD/Randall--Sundrum picture
rather than merely restating it.

\subsection{Recovery of known limits}
H1's scoped formal statement (\S\ref{subsec:h1}) reduces to Newtonian
gravity, $g=GM/r^2$, by construction: $\Sigma_0$ plays $G$'s role
exactly, with no new free parameter at this stage. That recovery is
not merely formal -- it has been checked numerically against five
independent bodies.

\paragraph{Five-body check: Earth, Mars, Sun, Jupiter, Saturn.} Define
$A := g/(H_0^2 R)$ for each body, using its measured surface gravity
$g$, radius $R$, and today's Hubble parameter $H_0\approx
2.18\times10^{-18}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. If elasticity contributed
anything beyond ordinary Newtonian gravity, $A$ would need an
independent, per-body explanation. Instead:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\hline
Body & $\rho$ (kg/m$^3$) & $g$ (m/s$^2$) & $A=g/(H_0^2R)$ \\ \hline
Earth & 5514 & 9.81 & $3.24\times10^{29}$ \\
Mars & 3933 & 3.71 & $2.30\times10^{29}$ \\
Sun & 1408 & 274 & $8.29\times10^{28}$ \\
Jupiter & 1326 & 24.79 & $7.46\times10^{28}$ \\
Saturn & 687 & 10.44 & $3.77\times10^{28}$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
$A$ is not constant -- it spans a factor of $\sim$8.6 across these
five bodies -- but it is not independent new physics either: it
tracks each body's density \emph{exactly}, since algebraically
$A=(4\pi G/3H_0^2)\rho$, with $H_0^2$ cancelling completely once $A$
is multiplied back through to recover $g$. The ratio $A/A_\oplus$
matches $\rho/\rho_\oplus$ to within a few percent for every body in
this table (the residual is measurement rounding and non-sphericity
for the gas giants, not a missing term). \textbf{Conclusion:} $H_0$
plays no role in local gravity at all, and every number here is
exactly what $g=GM/r^2$ already predicts -- the recovery of the
Newtonian limit is confirmed, with zero residual left for elasticity
to explain beyond $G$'s own, already-measured value.

\paragraph{Shell-theorem check.} H1's claim that bend depends on mass
and distance, not density and radius independently
(\S\ref{subsec:h1}, point 2), was checked directly: Earth's actual
mass and radius give $g=2.454\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at $r=1.274\times10^7$
m; a hypothetical body of the same mass but radius equal to that same
$r$ (density $\approx689\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$) gives the identical
$g=2.454\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at its own surface. Density and radius carry
no information beyond what $M$ and $r$ already fix.

\paragraph{Birkhoff's theorem as a bound on $\Sigma$ and on fluidity.}
Birkhoff's theorem (1923) shows that spherical symmetry plus the
\emph{vacuum} field equations with a fixed coupling $\Sigma_0$ force
the exterior field of any non-rotating mass to be static and exactly
Schwarzschild -- a result that makes no reference to, and needs no
input from, the kinematic triplet $(\theta,\sigma_{\mu\nu},
\omega_{\mu\nu})$ of \S\ref{subsec:core_terms}: those describe how
observers move through a spacetime once it is fixed, not an ingredient
in fixing it. The theorem's premises, however, assume exactly what
H1 and H2 propose to relax: a constant $\Sigma$, and no curvature
contribution from expansion or spin beyond what mass-energy already
supplies through the Einstein equations. If $\Sigma(x)$ varies, or if
$\theta$ or $\omega_{\mu\nu}$ contribute to curvature independently
of local mass-energy, Birkhoff's conclusion is no longer guaranteed:
the field around an isolated, non-rotating mass need not stay static
or purely Schwarzschild in form. This is a real, independent
consistency requirement, not a restatement of the $\dot G/G$ and
cosmic-rotation bounds already noted in \S\ref{subsec:h1} and
\S\ref{subsec:h2}: the solar system's gravitational field has itself
been measured to extraordinary precision (planetary ranging, light
bending, perihelion precession) and matches the static
Schwarzschild/Kerr prediction with no detected extra time-dependence
or unexpected multipole structure. \textbf{Any variation proposed for
$\Sigma$, and any curvature sourced by $\theta$ or $\omega_{\mu\nu}$
beyond the mass-energy term in the Raychaudhuri equation, must be
small enough in the solar-system regime to leave Birkhoff's
static, Schwarzschild-shaped conclusion intact there} -- in addition
to, not instead of, matching the measured value and constancy of
$\Sigma_0$ itself.

\subsection{Dimensional analysis and units}
\textbf{$\Sigma$.} $\Sigma_0 = c^4/8\pi G$: $[c^4]=\mathrm{m^4/s^4}$,
$[G]=\mathrm{m^3/(kg\,s^2)}$, so $[\Sigma_0] =
\mathrm{(m^4/s^4)\cdot(kg\,s^2/m^3)} = \mathrm{kg\,m/s^2} =
\mathrm{N}$ -- a force, exactly matching the already-standard
``stiffness of spacetime'' reading of this constant
(\S\ref{subsec:core_terms}) and consistent throughout H1/H1b.

\textbf{$g=GM/r^2$.} $[GM]=\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$, divided by
$[r^2]=\mathrm{m^2}$ gives $\mathrm{m/s^2}$, an acceleration -- checks
out in every numeric table in this section.

\textbf{$\mathcal{I}(x)$ -- an item this check surfaced and that has
since been corrected at the definition.} $\theta$, $\sigma_{\mu\nu}$,
$\omega_{\mu\nu}$ each carry units of inverse time (an
expansion/shear/rotation \emph{rate}), so $(\theta-\bar\theta)^2$ has
units $\mathrm{s^{-2}}$. For $\mathcal{I}:=\ell^2\sqrt{(\theta-
\bar\theta)^2+\cdots}$ (\S\ref{subsec:core_terms}) to come out
dimensionless as claimed, $\ell^2$ must carry units of
$\mathrm{s^2}$ -- i.e.\ $\ell$ is a reference \emph{time}, not a
length, despite the symbol -- and \S\ref{subsec:core_terms}'s
definition now states this explicitly rather than calling $\ell$ a
length scale. \S\ref{sec:notation}'s broader units convention is
still an open placeholder, so $\ell$'s specific numerical value (what
time scale, fixed by what physical consideration) remains open and
should be settled before $\mathcal{I}$ is used quantitatively in H1b
or the taxonomy (\S\ref{subsec:taxonomy}) -- but the dimensional
inconsistency itself, between calling $\ell$ a length at its
definition and needing it to be a time here, is resolved.

\subsection{Known observational constraints}
\textbf{Passed (positive recoveries, not just non-contradictions).}
H1's scoped formal statement matches two independent, high-precision
measurements exactly: the GPS gravitational time-dilation correction
($\sim45.7\,\mu\mathrm{s}$/day, from $\Delta(GM_\oplus/rc^2)$ between
ground and satellite altitude) and the solar gravitational redshift
($\approx2.12\times10^{-6}$, from $GM_\odot/R_\odot c^2$). Neither
required any adjustment to reproduce.

\textbf{Constraints $\Sigma$ (constant) already satisfies.}
Lunar-laser-ranging and pulsar-timing bounds on $\dot G/G$ (parts in
$10^{11}$--$10^{12}$ per year) directly bound H1's constant-$\Sigma$
claim (\S\ref{subsec:h1}, point 1) and are satisfied by construction,
since H1 proposes no variation. The Cassini Shapiro-delay bound on
the Brans--Dicke parameter ($\omega>40{,}000$; Bertotti, Iess \&
Tortora, 2003) is the relevant constraint on H1b specifically
(\S\ref{subsec:h1b}): any $\lambda\,h(\mathcal{I})$ term must stay
within whatever this bound translates to in the solar-system regime,
where $\mathcal{I}$ is small.

\textbf{A constraint an earlier version of this framework failed, and
was revised in response.} An amplification factor tying local gravity
to $H_0$ directly was tested against Big Bang nucleosynthesis: the
Hubble parameter at BBN ($\sim0.5\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$) exceeds $H_0$ by
a factor of $\sim2.3\times10^{17}$, so a gravity term scaling as
$H^2$ would have been $\sim5\times10^{34}$ times stronger at BBN --
grossly inconsistent with the observed light-element abundances, which
match standard $G$ to within $\sim$10--20\%. This is why $H_0$ does
not appear in H1's final formal statement: it cancels out of the
recovered formula algebraically in any case
(\S\ref{sec:consistency}.1), and taking it as a literal, real-time
ingredient is independently ruled out by this bound. Recorded here as
a constraint the hypothesis's earlier form failed and was corrected
against, not one it currently needs to satisfy.

\textbf{Constraints not yet checked.} Equivalence-principle
(E\"otv\"os-type) tests, Mercury's perihelion precession, and
gravitational-wave speed/waveform constraints all bear on H2's and
H1b's claims specifically and have not yet been worked through
numerically; flagged here as the next items for this subsection.
Light bending, which belonged on this list through the previous
revision, has since been evaluated numerically and removed from it:
H2c's deflection split, evaluated for the Sun, reproduces the
measured $1.75''$ solar deflection (\S\ref{subsec:h2}'s numeric check,
immediately following H2c's formal statement) -- the standard GR
formula transcribed and evaluated, not an independent derivation from
$\Sigma$.

\subsection{Internal consistency}
\textbf{Density is not an independent degree of freedom alongside
elasticity.} The five-body table in \S\ref{sec:consistency}.1 shows
$A=(4\pi G/3H_0^2)\rho$ exactly -- the quantity that seemed to vary
body-to-body is density itself, relabeled, not an independent
confirmation that ``elasticity'' is doing new explanatory work. This
is logged here as a resolved internal-consistency question for H1:
constant $\Sigma$ plus varying $\rho$ fully accounts for every number
checked, with nothing left over.

\textbf{``Bend'' is ambiguous between two quantities with different
shapes, and H1's language should specify which.} Gravitational force/
acceleration falls as $1/r^2$; gravitational time dilation (the
potential) falls as $1/r$. These are not the same curve: at a 1\%
threshold of surface value, the force well closes at $10\,R_\oplus$
while the time well remains open out to $100\,R_\oplus$, and the gap
between them grows linearly with $r$ (the ratio is $r/R_\oplus$
itself). H1's informal statement (``the amount of bend'') should be
read as the force/curvature sense by default, consistent with its
formal statement, with the time-dilation sense flagged as a distinct
quantity sourced by the same $M$ whenever both are discussed together
(as in H2's thought experiment, \S\ref{subsec:h2}).

\textbf{Absolute well depth and local gradient are different
questions, and a hypothesis using ``influence'' needs to say which it
means.} Earth's own time-dilation depth is smaller than the Sun's
ambient depth at Earth's orbit by a factor of $\sim$14 -- by absolute
depth, Earth's influence effectively ends within its own radius. Yet
Earth's \emph{gradient} (the quantity GPS clocks and tidal forces
actually respond to) dominates the Sun's gradient by a factor of
$\sim$300 at satellite altitude, and remains dominant out to the Hill
sphere ($\sim1.5\times10^6$ km, $\sim235\,R_\oplus$) -- the same
boundary recovered independently from a tidal-balance argument
($GM_\oplus/r^2 = 2GM_\odot r/a^3$). H2 and H8's ``sinking into
space/time'' language should be read as a gradient claim, not an
absolute-depth claim, given this result; stated here so later
revisions of H2/H8 inherit the distinction rather than re-deriving it.

\textbf{Compactness has a sharp internal-consistency boundary, already
established, that connects H1 directly to H8.} Compressing a fixed
mass $M$ into a smaller radius $R$ leaves the field unchanged for any
$r>R_{\mathrm{old}}$ (shell theorem) but raises the surface value as
$1/R^2$ and the surface time-dilation depth as $1/R$ -- a steeper,
closer-in bend, with elasticity itself untouched throughout. This
process has a hard stop: once $R\le R_s=2GM/c^2$ (for the Sun,
$R_s\approx3$ km; worked Earth-mass example in \S\ref{subsec:h1}
discussion gives $R_s\approx8.9$ mm), no pressure -- not thermal, not
electron- or neutron-degeneracy -- can balance gravity any further,
which is exactly the Chandrasekhar/TOV boundary already catalogued in
\S\ref{sec:foundations}.1. This is the precise, checked link between
H1's compaction result and H8's collapse narrative: H1 describes the
approach to $R_s$ as a smooth tightening of the bend; H8 describes
what happens once $R_s$ is reached. Logged here so H8's eventual
revision can cite this derivation rather than re-deriving the
threshold.

    field, this paper's own numbers (\S\ref{subsec:h2}; $cH_0$
    roughly ten orders of magnitude short of $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$,
    and $H_0^2 R\approx3\times10^{-29}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ -- $H_0^2$
    itself is a rate, s$^{-2}$, not an acceleration, so it is
    converted via Earth's radius $R$ before comparing to $g$ --
    roughly thirty orders short) rule the engine out entirely.
    \textbf{H2-revised} (curvature itself as the cause, kept) leaves
    no prediction to state beyond the geodesic equation's existing,
    already-standard $-\nabla\Phi$ field -- i.e., no prediction
    beyond GR survives from either version of H2 as currently
    written.
  \item \textbf{H6.} An explicit $v(r)$ derived from the proposed
    vortex's strength and reach, plotted against a measured rotation
    curve at $r\sim10\,\mathrm{kpc}$, together with an explicit
    statement of what the same mechanism predicts for the Bullet
    Cluster's mass/gas offset. Absent both, H6 makes no prediction
    distinguishable from a renamed dark-matter halo.
  \item \textbf{H7.} A specific relation between particle/energy
    creation and $\theta$ that reproduces the Parker or Hawking rate
    in its respective established limit, with no net-energy-
    extraction term -- the unification claim stated as a formula, not
    only as a motivation (\S\ref{subsec:h7}).
  \item \textbf{H8.} $R_{\mathrm{core}}(\tau)$'s minimum set by a
    stated physical scale (as Planck stars and fuzzballs each already
    do for their own proposals), and the vortex's strength stated as
    proportional to the progenitor's own angular momentum, vanishing
    identically when that spin is zero.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% =====================================================================
\subsection{What this paper does and does not claim}
This paper does not claim to have found a new source of gravity, a
new source of energy, or a replacement for dark matter. Where a
hypothesis's own numeric check came out against it (H2's magnitude
and shape problems, \S\ref{subsec:h2}; H6's scale mismatch,
\S\ref{subsec:h6}; H4's tension with interior-structure data,
\S\ref{subsec:h4}), that result is accepted here as a result, not
argued around. What this paper does claim is narrower: that treating
space as a real medium with elasticity, fluidity, and irregularity
(P1--P3, \S\ref{sec:foundations}) is a coherent starting point that
recovers standard gravity exactly where it must (H1, scoped;
\S\ref{sec:consistency}), correctly identifies several genuinely open
questions in current astrophysics (the SMBH seed problem behind H3/H5;
the hierarchy problem behind H9; the vacuum-energy-conversion pattern
behind H7), and in at least one case (H8) restates already-serious
GR content more plainly than it is usually presented. The value on
offer, honestly, is mostly in having specific numbers to check against
-- several of which came out negative -- not in a finished alternative
to GR.

\paragraph{Kept, as live and developed claims.}
\textbf{H2a}, in its revised form (\textbf{H2-revised} in
\S\ref{sec:predictions}'s labeling): gravity as the felt consequence
of spacetime curvature itself (\S\ref{subsec:h2}), with the widening
gap between neighboring local clock rates as curvature deepens
constituting the acceleration ($a=-\partial_r\Phi$, the Newtonian
limit of the geodesic equation) -- consistent with the GPS
correction, Chou et al.'s clock data run in reverse, and Earth's
tidal gradient, and adopted as this paper's defining statement of
gravity, with the explicit caveat that those three checks are one
potential, $\Phi=-GM/r$ for Earth, read three ways, not three
independent confirmations, and that the statement itself is a
scoped recovery of standard GR rather than content beyond it.
\textbf{H2b}, kept specifically as the
demonstration of what this definition excludes: a centrifugal driver
reproduces the same felt sensation but carries zero true,
frame-independent curvature, and so does not qualify as gravity under
H2a's own definition. \textbf{H1}, scoped exactly as \S\ref{subsec:h1}
states it: as vocabulary and a checked recovery, not yet as
independent content.
\textbf{H7}, as a unification proposal aimed at $\theta$, with the
net-extraction claim explicitly dropped
(\S\ref{sec:predictions}). \textbf{H8}, as the strongest hypothesis in
the set, with its two open items (angular momentum, $R_{\mathrm{core}}$'s
scale) named rather than assumed away. \textbf{H9}, kept only as a
citation of an existing, already-bounded program (ADD/Randall--Sundrum)
and the connection it offers to $\Sigma_0$ -- not as a new mechanism.

\paragraph{Parked or substantially withdrawn.}
\textbf{H2a's original cosmic-expansion engine} specifically (not H2a
as now revised, kept above): as a global origin story for Earth's (or
any body's) own field, the magnitude and shape objections in
\S\ref{subsec:h2}, sharpened in \S\ref{sec:predictions}, are decisive
as that version was stated. What remains viable from it is only the
much narrower, already-standard river-model statement that space
flows \emph{locally} toward a mass -- which predicts nothing beyond
GR, and which curvature-as-cause (H2a's revised form) now supersedes
rather than needs. \textbf{H4}'s literal claim, ``a black hole at the center of every
body,'' is directly contradicted by existing helioseismological,
seismological, and geodetic data (\S\ref{subsec:h4}) and is withdrawn
in that form; the only reading not already ruled out is the
micro-depression row of the taxonomy with no horizon, at which point
H4 is ordinary gravitational instability under a new name.
\textbf{H6}'s claim that its central vortex reaches the scale where
flat rotation curves are measured is withdrawn as stated (a
three-to-four-order-of-magnitude scale mismatch,
\S\ref{subsec:h6}); the target observation remains real and open.

\paragraph{Narrowed to a question, not a mechanism.}
\textbf{H3} and \textbf{H5} survive only as restatements of the
genuine, open supermassive-black-hole seed problem, to be checked
against the mainstream benchmarks (direct collapse, heavy seeds,
super-Eddington accretion; stellar-mass seeds plus mergers) they
currently compete with rather than build on -- not as literal tears
in, or literal collisions of, spacetime's fabric, for which no
stress-energy source or falsifiable signature has yet been stated.

\subsection{Open problems and known weaknesses}
The strongest objection to each hypothesis, and its current status,
collected from \S\ref{sec:foundations} and \S\ref{sec:hypotheses}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{H1} -- not yet falsifiable on its own (constant
    $\Sigma$ makes no prediction beyond GR). \emph{Open}, pending H1b.
  \item \textbf{H1b} -- $h$ and $\lambda$ unspecified; Birkhoff's
    theorem means any resolution is an amendment to the field
    equations, not a parameter. \emph{Open, and not yet written as
    the amendment it would have to be.}
  \item \textbf{H2-engine} (the retired cosmic-expansion/spin driver)
    -- magnitude ($\sim10$ orders short for $cH_0$, $\sim30$ orders
    short for $H_0^2R$) and shape (uniform driver vs.\
    mass-centered, inverse-square field) objections are decisive as
    stated. \emph{Answered, in the negative.} \textbf{H2-revised}
    (curvature itself as the cause, kept as this paper's defining
    statement; \S\ref{subsec:h2}) is a different claim from
    H2-engine and is not touched by that negative answer -- it is
    \emph{not} answered in the negative, but it is also not a
    prediction beyond GR: it is the standard $-\nabla\Phi$ recovery,
    \emph{open} only in the sense that it does not, by itself,
    explain why mass curves spacetime this way in the first place.
  \item \textbf{H3} -- no stated mechanism distinguishable from
    direct-collapse seeding. \emph{Open.}
  \item \textbf{H4} -- literal claim contradicted by interior-
    structure data. \emph{Answered, in the negative,} for bodies
    where that data exists.
  \item \textbf{H5} -- survives the Bullet Cluster test only by
    positing near-frictionless space-collision, and lacks a stated
    stress-energy source. \emph{Open, on both counts.}
  \item \textbf{H6} -- scale mismatch between the vortex's reach and
    the observation it targets. \emph{Answered, in the negative,} as
    stated; MOND's own split between rotation-curve success and
    cluster/CMB difficulty is the benchmark any revision should expect
    to face.
  \item \textbf{H7} -- magnitude gap (32 orders of magnitude) at the
    present epoch, and no stated mechanism for net extraction.
    \emph{Answered} on extraction (dropped); magnitude gap is
    expected, not a flaw, given the mechanism.
  \item \textbf{H8} -- angular momentum and $R_{\mathrm{core}}$'s
    minimum both unstated. \emph{Open,} but narrowly -- both are
    additions to an otherwise well-grounded picture, not challenges
    to it.
  \item \textbf{H9} -- $n$ and $\ell_\ast$ unspecified, and already
    tightly bounded ($\lesssim40$--$50\,\mu\mathrm{m}$) by existing
    tests. \emph{Open, within a narrow remaining window.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================
This paper proposed treating space as a real, elastic, fluid medium
(H1, H1b) and, from that single departure, reframed gravity as the
felt consequence of spacetime curvature -- the widening gap between
local clock rates at neighboring points as curvature deepens --
rather than as an added force (H2a), with H2b and H2c each showing
why a spatial bend alone and a flat-spacetime rotational analogy
cannot do that job on their own. Pressed against a further cluster of
open astrophysical questions (H3--H9), this departure survived some
of its own numeric checks (H1's inverse-square recovery, H2c's match
to the measured solar light-bending angle) and failed others outright
(H2a's original cosmic-expansion engine, H4's literal claim, H6's
scale claim), each resolved here in the direction its own numbers
pointed rather than defended or argued around (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
What remains, after that filtering, is not offered as a replacement
for general relativity -- H2a-revised is GR's own geodesic equation
in its Newtonian limit, not content beyond it, and H3, H5, H7, H8, and
H9 still owe the specific, falsifiable form \S\ref{sec:predictions}
asks of them -- but as a disciplined set of working hypotheses, each
stated plainly enough to be checked and, where checked, either kept,
narrowed, or withdrawn on its own merits. Mercury's perihelion
precession and gravitational-wave speed/waveform constraints are the
next, specific numeric items this framework still owes
(\S\ref{sec:consistency}).
