\paragraph{Status vocabulary.}
\textbf{CLOSED} --- resolved and merged into the paper.
\textbf{CLOSED (redirected)} --- determined not to be the paper's
question; destination named.

\emph{Status: R6, closed (redirected).}

Proposed: besides information vectors (capped, $r_i\le1$), an atom
may carry meta vectors describing it rather than being part of its

Meta vectors are not capped. Split by destination: the taxonomy and

idempotence), so $N$ and $D$ keep the mass-number/atomic-number

\subsection*{v15 --- when two metrics constrain each other}

(a non-redundant reporting set, a two-axis placement, a debugging
check, a post-hoc label for Assembly's camps) are speculative.

\subsection*{v17 --- Gap 2 resolved}
Meta vector, not sort, tested against the cap: $r\le1$ encodes

\subsection*{v19 --- pure and mixed atoms, cores, and Collapse against a bowl}
Prompted by a hypothetical: could the Mona Lisa be held in one atom?
``One atom'' can mean three things. \emph{A name}: a single seated
point, like ``storm,'' which the paper calls a doorbell --- it carries
none of the content. \emph{A bowl}: already legal for mixed sorts,
since Bundle is sort-blind. \emph{A Collapsed atom} holding colors,
words, and strokes, which needs mixed sorts inside one atom. This is
the example v3 left unresolved (``a whole painting as a single
vector'' was Gap~1 restated). With Gap~1 closed, a single
\emph{vector} is refused, while one atom of many vectors stays
available. What stands in the way of the third meaning: mixed sorts
(R20, widened to R36); where each contribution sits on the canvas ---
a location per contribution, the same kind of register as v17's but
not yet specified per contribution; derived quantities across sorts;
and relations, since the paper ``does not mint the relations'' and,
by its own design rule, which units were co-present and within what
region are facts of a level, not coordinates of an atom.

\pagebreak[4]
\textbf{Rulings given in the discussion, none yet in the paper.}\par\nopagebreak
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Collapse juxtaposes.} Positions do not change, nothing
merges, no new type is minted. Relations within a sort are as before;
across sorts they exist only under a stated rule (Axiom~0's
``undecided, not admitted,'' applied to Collapse). So derived
quantities that use direction are computed per sort, and cross-sort ones
are undefined until a rule exists. (Refined in v20: strength-only
quantities may also be given for the whole atom.)
\item \emph{Cores.} A core is a unit inside an atom, usually one
$\tau$ (a pure type) but free to hold several --- words and numbers
together --- as typical, not as a rule. The general form is
per-vector $\tau_i$, with ``core'' a grouping. ``Core'' appears in
the paper only in the ``Core Contributions'' heading and in ``no
core, no halo'' in the enclosure, so it needs one defined sense.
\item \emph{Collapse is not fixed at one vector per contribution.}
This settles the question the paper was silent on: whether collapsing
already-multi-vector atoms flattens or nests. The Collapse text still
says ``one vector per contribution.''
\item \emph{$N$ is the number of cores.} This keeps UnCollapse's
$N\to N-1$, keeps $D$ a bowl size (distinct cores), and keeps the v17
registers at length $N$, so those registers are per core, not per
vector. The cost is a word collision: $N$ is currently the vector
count in Axiom~7, in $\gamma_{\text{atom}}=\mathcal V_{\text{atom}}/N$
and in $\sigma_{\text{atom}}$'s $N^2/(N-1)$. \emph{Decided:} $N$ counts cores and $Q$ counts vectors. All seven
Axiom~7 metrics run over vectors, so they take $Q$; the $D/N$
paragraph and the UnCollapse text keep $N$ and change ``vectors'' to
``cores.'' To settle at the merge: the sentence that defines $N$ as the
atom's count of informational vectors, and where the label ``info
number'' goes.
\item \emph{A Collapsed atom matches a counted bowl.} They are
identical exactly when nothing repeats ($D=N$), and $N-D$ is the gap.
The paper's bowl is a set, and its own counting equation shows the
difference: $A\uplus A\uplus A\uplus A\setminus A=\emptyset$, while
$A\oplus A\oplus A\oplus A\ominus A=A^{\langle3\rangle}$ (by the
UnCollapse rule). Operations should work on both; recorded as a
commuting test --- a pack/unpack map between the two, with every
operation required to agree across it. Sum-style quantities
($S$, $\mathcal E$, $V_{\text{derived}}$) agree only on a counted
bowl. $T$ is asymmetric today: it ``does not act on the set $R$ as a
set,'' and a Collapsed atom can act only once a cast is named.
\item \emph{Two distinct ways of arranging and viewing vectors.}
They differ in multiplicity (set against counted), in handle (a
Collapsed atom is one unit that can sit in a bowl or at a lexicon
pin; a bowl is a collection), and in sequence (neither stores order;
the path carries it, and a bowl is a good surface for displaying one).
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{v20 --- energy from strength, counts, and direction; effective counts}
Started from a recollection that energy is $\sum r^3$. The paper's
energy is $\mathcal E=\sum r^2$; $\sum r^3$ is the atom's volume,
formerly ``mass.'' Nothing needs renaming, and $\sum r^3$ keeps a
geometric reading (strength as a radius, as in Assembly's softening
length).

\textbf{Energy from strength, vector count $Q$, and direction already
exists in $\mathcal E$.} Three exact relations, verified on 20{,}000
random atoms (errors near $10^{-14}$, no violations), with
$S=\sum r_i$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathcal E=S^2/Q+Q\,\mathrm{Var}(r)$: an evenly spread floor,
set by $S$ and $Q$ alone, plus concentration.
\item $S^2/Q\le\mathcal E\le S$.
\item $|V_{\text{derived}}|^2=(1-\alpha)\mathcal E+\alpha S^2$: the
resultant's energy, blending the incoherent baseline with the fully
aligned value, weighted by the paper's coherence $\alpha$.
\end{itemize}
Which works better: the floor $S^2/Q$ cannot tell an even atom from a
concentrated one (both give $1.000$ at $S=2$, $Q=4$, where $\mathcal E$
gives $1.000$ against $1.810$). $\mathcal V=\sum r^3$ separates them even
more sharply ($0.500$ against $1.715$) but has no identity tying it to
anything beyond the radius reading. So $\mathcal E$ is the energy and
$\mathcal V$ stays volume. Cores $N$ enter through the same
variance-of-a-sum algebra applied per core (plain algebra, not run
separately) and through the ratios $Q/N$ and $S/N$. The mean strength
$S/Q$, the parked candidate from v13 (then written $S/N$), is the centre
of the variance decomposition and keeps a plain name.

\textbf{Effective vector count} $Q_{\mathrm{eff}}=S^2/\mathcal E$
(the participation ratio in physics, the effective sample size in
statistics).
\begin{itemize}
\item $Q$ counts vectors and $Q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ counts those that matter:
100 vectors at $0.05$ give $100$; one vector at $0.9$ plus 99 at
$0.001$ give $1.23$.
\item The largest vector carries at least $1/Q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of the
strength (50{,}000 random atoms, no violations).
\item Scale-free: halving every $r$ leaves it unchanged ($2.2099$ both).
\item With direction: $|V|^2/S^2=\alpha+(1-\alpha)/Q_{\mathrm{eff}}$
(error near $7\times10^{-16}$), so two dimensionless numbers fix the
resultant's size.
\item Limits: it re-expresses $S$ and $\mathcal E$, adding no
information, and ignores direction.
\item Overlap: $\sigma_{\text{atom}}=(Q/Q_{\mathrm{eff},\mathcal V}-1)/(Q-1)$,
where $Q_{\mathrm{eff},\mathcal V}$ is the effective count of the
\emph{cubed} shares (error near $10^{-15}$). So $\sigma_{\text{atom}}$
is already this family, and $Q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is its plain-strength
sibling, the one tied by identity to $\mathcal E$ and $|V|^2$. Whether
to keep both, one, or neither is undecided.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Per sort and for the whole.} The per-sort rule belongs to
quantities that use direction ($V_{\text{derived}}$, $\alpha$,
$|V|^2$), since sums and dot products of directions across sorts mean
nothing. Strength-only quantities ($S$, $\mathcal E$, $\mathcal V$,
$Q_{\mathrm{eff}}$, the $\sigma$'s) can be given per sort and for the
whole, on one stated convention: each sort's $r$ is normalized to its
own declared saturation (the paper's $r=a/a_{\max}$), and whole-atom
figures treat fraction of own saturation as a common scale. With
$w_s$ the share of total strength held by sort $s$:
\begin{itemize}
\item Pooling ratio $\sum_sQ_{\mathrm{eff},s}/Q_{\mathrm{eff,whole}}\ge1$
(Cauchy--Schwarz; no violations in 30{,}000 atoms), with equality
exactly when every sort has the same strength-weighted mean strength
$\mathcal E_s/S_s$. It measures imbalance between sorts: two vectors at
$0.9$ against ten at $0.1$ give $2.63$.
\item Exact decomposition:
$1/Q_{\mathrm{eff,whole}}=\sum_sw_s^2/Q_{\mathrm{eff},s}$ (error near
$2\times10^{-16}$). In the symmetric case it multiplies: three sorts of
four vectors at $0.5$ give $12=3\times4$.
\item Effective number of sorts $1/\sum_sw_s^2$ answers ``is this atom
really mixed?'' more readably than the ratio: for 50 color vectors at
$0.5$, 3 word vectors at $0.5$, and 10 stroke vectors at $0.3$,
$Q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is $50$, $3$, $10$ per sort, $61.5$ for the whole,
and the effective number of sorts is $1.37$. Three sorts are present but
the atom reads as about $1.4$, because color carries $85\%$ of the
strength.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Status: R41--R43; the v19 per-sort line is refined above.}

\subsection*{v23 --- meta vectors as specified, and what to settle before importing them}
Specification as given, parked here and not yet in the paper:
\begin{itemize}
\item Meta vectors are labels on coaches; information vectors are the
content in the coaches. Meta vectors are not capped at $1$.
\item Well-known ones: $V_{\text{derived}}$ (the sum of the vectors),
$V_{\text{timestamp}}$, $V_{\text{expiry}}$ ($0$ meaning no expiry),
$V_{\text{lastmodify}}$, $V_{\text{location}}$, and $V_{\text{link}}$
(forward, backward, bidirectional).
\item They give information atoms kinds that help group them for
specific tasks; the applications named are typing and sorting.
\item They can build data structures in Assembly (sequences, graphs,
layered clusters) and in Inference.
\item They are part of information atoms. Some change with content and
some do not.
\end{itemize}
Review of that specification against the paper's own text, with the
demonstrations run. Nothing in the paper changed.

\textbf{Conflicts with text already in the paper.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{G1, label identity.} $\uplus$, $D$ and UnCollapse compare
content. If a timestamp joins an atom's identity, every atom is unique by
birth time: three contributions $A$, $B$, $A$ give $D=2$ by content and
$D=3$ with timestamps, so $N-D$ is always $0$ and $A\uplus A\ne A$.
Equality must be content-only, and a rule is needed for which labels
survive when $\uplus$ merges two atoms of equal content.
\item \emph{G2, the level rule.} \S3.1 says ``a unit carries no
representation of what it constitutes. A seated word does not know the
poem,'' and that which units were co-present, over what interval, and
within what region are ``constituting facts of a level, not coordinates
of an atom,'' which is ``why the atom stays at four coordinates and one
sort tag.'' Time, place and links sit against this. A candidate
resolution: labels ride beside the four coordinates, as $V_{\text{derived}}$
already does, and a link may point to sources or peers but not up to what
the atom constitutes, since a word's forward link to its poem would break
the rule. The sentence needs rewording either way.
\item \emph{G3, the derived-vector limit.} Axiom~7 says ``at most one
further, derived vector may sit alongside them.'' A family of labels
contradicts it. Reword to at most one derived-from-content vector (one per
sort once R36 lands), with recorded labels separate, all excluded from $N$
and $Q$, and the seven metrics and $V_{\text{derived}}$ ranging over
informational vectors only. $V_{\text{derived}}=\sum r_i\hat n_i$ with
$\chi$ excluded, which ``the sum of the vectors'' abbreviates.
\item \emph{G4, mutation.} The paper has no in-place change: its verbs
act ``alongside, not in place of,'' and Collapse produces an additional
atom. $V_{\text{lastmodify}}$ and forward or bidirectional links assume an
atom that persists through change, which is the identity handle (R18,
not adopted). With immutable atoms only backward links are free, since a
newer atom can point to older sources, which is the provenance reasoning
the Collapse section already makes; $V_{\text{lastmodify}}$ then equals
the newer atom's $V_{\text{timestamp}}$. One world must be chosen.
\item \emph{G5, vocabulary.} ``Typed'' is in the paper's title, and
type/typed occur $21$ times and sort $88$ times. Meta vectors giving
atoms ``types,'' and ``sorting'' them, collide with $\tau$. Use another
word for the kind (label or class) and ``order'' or ``rank'' for the verb.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Gotchas in the logic.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{G6, timestamp encoding.} v17's clock-face vector gives the
identical vector for now, one day ago, and one week ago (checked), so age
and expiry cannot be read from it. They need an absolute, non-cyclic
time. This answers the earlier ``one component or two'': an absolute
scalar for age and expiry, and optionally the clock-face vector for
grouping by time of day.
\item \emph{G7, sentinels.} ``Expiry $0$ means none'' reads as already
expired under $\text{now}<\text{expiry}$ (checked), and a location of
$(0,0)$ is a real place. The paper's own idiom is that black is an empty
seat, so ``no expiry'' is the absence of $V_{\text{expiry}}$, and absence
is not zero.
\item \emph{G8, granularity and propagation.} Coach may mean atom or
core. Timestamp, expiry and link are per core (the R38 registers), while
$V_{\text{derived}}$ is per atom, and per sort once R36 lands. A Collapse
result is a new atom whose own birth differs from its cores' births, so
there are two time notions. Each verb also needs a stated rule for the
labels of its result; the proposal is that a result is new with its own
birth, core labels travel with their cores under Collapse, and links
record provenance.
\item \emph{G9, grouping needs a comparison per kind.} Sphere distance
for location, cyclic distance for the clock, equality for identifiers,
graph distance for links: no common metric, so each kind needs a stated
rule, the analogue of Axiom~0 for labels. Links are identifiers and not
coordinates, and calling labels vectors invites arithmetic; no verb acts
on a label, as none acts on the seat $\vec P$.
\item \emph{G10, two sources of structure.} Links declare structure,
while Assembly's energy produces camps from geometry and a seat; keep them
distinct. Link use belongs to Paper~2 (R16) and the taxonomy to Paper~1.
Links held in labels can also carry order past $\uplus$, so state that
$\uplus$ does not read labels, which keeps ``order comes from outside''
true of what $\uplus$ reads.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Status: R15 specified; R45 open.}


\subsection*{v24 --- rulings on the meta-vector questions}
Rulings given:
\begin{itemize}
\item Drop $V_{\text{lastmodify}}$. Atoms are immutable and change only
through Collapse and UnCollapse.
\item Meta vectors are not part of $D$.
\item Meta vectors link to other things mostly by criteria on other meta
vectors and never by content. Words are never linked to a poem: the words
do not know the poem, the path knows it.
\item The absence of $V_{\text{expiry}}$ means no expiry.
\item Coach means atom, very loosely. Information atoms and meta vectors
share the same cores, a core being a single point whose weight varies with
the $N$ count; $V_{\text{derived}}$ has a magnitude and a direction that
help in higher dimensions.
\item Meta vectors help guide information atoms in higher dimensions, a
useful attribute in Assembly and Inference.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What each ruling settles, checked against the paper.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{G1, label identity.} Equality is content-only: two cores
coincide when they hold the same vectors, and labels do not enter $D$, so
they do not enter $\uplus$'s equality or UnCollapse's value-exactness
either. What $\uplus$ does with the labels of merged atoms remains (G14).
\item \emph{G2, the level rule.} It stands unchanged. Links run from label
to label, and a link never carries membership in a larger whole, which is
the paper's own rule that a seated word does not know the poem.
\item \emph{G4, mutation.} Immutable atoms make backward links the free
ones, and $V_{\text{lastmodify}}$ is dropped. One wording consequence: the
paper says UnCollapse ``removes one core from a Collapsed atom,'' which
reads as mutation. It returns a new atom without that core and leaves the
original unchanged, a one-line fix at import.
\item \emph{G7, sentinels.} Absence is not zero, and the rule extends to
every label. Validity is then: valid exactly when $V_{\text{expiry}}$ is
absent or $\text{now}<V_{\text{expiry}}$.
\item \emph{G8, granularity.} Shared cores make labels per core, matching
the R38 registers. $V_{\text{derived}}$ exists per core and per atom, and
the atom's is exactly the sum of its cores', since
$\sum_i r_i\hat n_i$ regroups by core.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{New points the rulings raise.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{G11, a core as a point with weight.} A multi-vector core
spans several directions, so the single point is its $V_{\text{derived}}$,
not its content. The weight at a point is the multiplicity $m(c)$, the
number of equal cores there, with $N=\sum_cm(c)$, $D$ the number of
distinct cores, and $N-D=\sum_c(m(c)-1)$. Call it multiplicity, not
density, which R12 retired, and keep it a property of the atom's list and
not of the core: a core does not know its siblings.
\item \emph{G12, $V_{\text{derived}}$ as a guide.} The paper already says
operators needing one pair read the derived sum as a weight or a
direction, and that beyond three dimensions it cannot be $T$'s actor,
which needs $N_{\mathrm{dim}}-2$ orthonormal arrows. So guidance means
read-only roles: a representative weight or direction, a seat, grouping.
Its reliability is atom coherence, $|V_{\text{derived}}|/S_{\text{atom}}$,
which is $0$ for an equal antipodal pair although $S>0$, leaving the
direction undefined. The verified third candidate of v1, disjoint
dimensions, which commute exactly and keep content recoverable, is the
available mechanism for labels to occupy their own dimensions without
disturbing content.
\item \emph{G13, links.} Links based on label criteria are derived at read
time. Only backward provenance, identity-based since the identifier is
itself a label (R18), is stored at birth. Forward and bidirectional are
views from a reverse index, and the three $V_{\text{link}}$ names become
view directions.
\item \emph{G14, $\uplus$ and labels.} $\uplus$ discards order and count,
so it naturally discards per-copy labels too, while Collapse keeps every
copy with its labels, which is the per-core register. This is the proposed
default and is unconfirmed. It also resolves R19 by proposal: filtering by
expiry is UnCollapse of the expired cores, a selection by label criterion,
so Mix and Collapse need not know about expiry.
\end{itemize}
The paper already holds two meta vectors without the name: $V_{\text{derived}}$
and the seat $\vec P$, which is uncapped, sits outside the four-tuple, and
enters $d_{AB}=|\vec P_B-\vec P_A|$. Importing the taxonomy therefore
mostly names what is there, and $V_{\text{location}}$ may turn out to be
the seat.

Still open before import: the wording of the derived-vector limit (G3),
the word for the kind (G5), the timestamp as an absolute scalar with an
optional clock-face vector (G6, unconfirmed), the comparison rule per kind
(G9), and the two sources of structure (G10).
\emph{Status: G1, G2, G4, G7, G8 settled; G11--G14 proposed.}


\subsection*{v25 --- confirmations, a check of the assembly claim, and bowls keeping labels}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{G11.} Confirmed: the word is multiplicity, used loosely.
\item \emph{G13.} The first part is confirmed: links by label criteria
are derived at read time. The second part is accepted on this
explanation: an immutable older atom cannot be edited to record a later
one, so it never stores a forward pointer. ``What was $A$ used in?'' is
answered by searching for atoms whose backward link names $A$, and that
search result is the forward view.
\item \emph{G12, refined and checked.} $V_{\text{derived}}$ is the sum of
an atom's vectors, with a magnitude and a direction that assist it in
higher dimensions, and the stated expectation is that atoms mostly
assemble with others of similar magnitude and direction. Checked against
the assembly energy $U_{AB}=-\tfrac{r_Ar_B}{2}(3+\hat n_A\!\cdot\!\hat n_B)
/(d_{AB}^2+\varepsilon_{AB}^2)^{3/2}$, $\varepsilon_{AB}=r_A+r_B$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Direction: supported.} Aligned pairs pull exactly twice as
strongly as opposed ones at every separation (ratio $2.000$ at $d=0$,
$0.5$, $1$, $3$), and in a random population of $300$ atoms the best
partner averages $12^\circ$ to $19^\circ$ away against about $90^\circ$
for a random partner.
\item \emph{Magnitude: conditional.} For an atom of $r=0.60$ the best
partner has $r=0.30$ at contact ($d=0$), $0.42$ at $d=0.5$, $0.69$ at
$d=1$, and $1.00$ at $d=3$. Only at fixed total strength do balanced
pairs win, with $|U|$ at $d=1$ of $0.1768$ for $(0.5,0.5)$, $0.1485$ for
$(0.7,0.3)$ and $0.0636$ for $(0.9,0.1)$. In the population the best
partner's strength gap is smaller than a random partner's at $d=0$ and
$d=1$ ($0.27$ and $0.24$ against $0.32$) and larger at $d=5$ ($0.42$
against $0.31$).
\end{itemize}
So direction is a clean grouping rule, while magnitude is a weight whose
effect depends on separation relative to the units' own extents. Two
cautions: $|V_{\text{derived}}|$ is uncapped and grows with $Q$ and with
multiplicity, so a many-vector atom is heavy in the product and in
$\varepsilon_{AB}$; and the direction is undefined at cancellation, where
atom coherence is $0$. This is a candidate for R22: read an atom's single
$(r,\hat n)$ from $V_{\text{derived}}$, which the paper already allows
for operators that need one pair.
\item \emph{G14, revised.} A bowl may keep labels as well, since labels
only identify the nature of atoms and $\uplus$ has no reason to read them;
they stay available to anything that wishes to. Consequence: $\uplus$
still discards count in what it reads, and content-level quantities see
one seat, but if per-copy labels ride along, count survives in them
whenever the labels differ. The pack and unpack map between a bowl and a
Collapsed atom (R39) is then lossless exactly when per-copy labels are
distinct, for instance identifiers. Bowl and Collapse stay nearly
identical but not the same: repeats enter a Collapsed atom's content, so
$S$, $\mathcal{E}$ and $V_{\text{derived}}$ see them, and they do not
enter a bowl's. Proposed default, unconfirmed: labels carried on a seat of
equal content merge as a set, equal values coinciding and distinct ones
both kept.
\item \emph{G5, G6, defaults proposed.} The paper already uses ``label''
in the sense meant (``direction is a label here,'' ``a label on the
relation''), so the individual meta vector is a label and atoms are
grouped by their labels, with no new noun and no ``type''; ``ordering''
replaces ``sorting.'' If a noun is wanted, ``category'' has zero uses in
the paper, against ``class'' with four and ``kind'' with ten. The
timestamp is an absolute scalar for age and expiry, with the clock-face
vector optional for grouping by time of day.
\end{itemize}

defaults proposed; import awaiting go-ahead.}

\subsection*{v27 --- rulings on form, expiry, Collapse and identity; fixes 1 to 3 merged}
Rulings given:
\begin{itemize}
\item Meta vectors are always vectors. Where one must act as a scalar it
may exist in $\mathbb{R}^2$, and standard planes (horizontal and vertical)
are wanted as recommendations for the various scalar vectors, with a
registry for scalars as the idea.
\item $V_{\text{derived}}$ always rests on information-content strength.
If an atom has an expiry, all its meta vectors become meaningless and it
no longer takes part in any process. If an inner atom of a larger
Collapsed atom expires, all its information and meta vectors stop
participating.
\item A larger Collapsed atom collects all its inner atoms' information
vectors at standard position and their meta vectors. $V_{\text{derived}}$
is recalculated over the denser content. The result has a new timestamp
vector and also holds the timestamps linked to each core. Neither
information vectors nor meta vectors ever lose identity, under Collapse,
Bundle or Mix.
\item Meta vectors are per core and, like information atoms, do not lose
identity. If an atom expires, all its information and meta vectors stop
contributing.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Merged in v15\_26, the three fixes asked for.}
The ``at most one'' derived vector in Axiom~7 now reads one per core and
one for the atom, the atom's being the sum of its cores'; the section's
``part of identity'' now says ``part of the value,'' and separates value
from the identity handle, which tells sources apart; and granularity is
stated for location and link as well, with a paragraph on where meta
vectors are held, including a Collapse result's own new timestamp and
backward link beside its cores'. That last paragraph also answers the open
question of an atom's birth against its cores' births.

\textbf{What the rulings raise, checked.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{R46, planes and a registry.} A registry fits as a further
catalogue in the paper's deferred-catalogues subsection, which already
holds the concept dictionary and the lexicon as recorded and left open, not
an axiom; it says ``a pair of catalogues'' but lists three items, the
third not a catalogue. Recommendations only, as standardization of the
lexicon is. On geometry: two planes inside a $3$-D stage always share a
line (checked on $20{,}000$ random pairs), so ``horizontal and vertical''
planes cannot be disjoint there. Disjoint planes need extra dimensions
($N_{\mathrm{dim}}\ge3+2k$ for $k$ planes; coordinate planes $(x_1,x_2)$
and $(x_3,x_4)$ in $\mathbb{R}^4$ meet only at the origin), which is the
mechanism of v1's third candidate, shifts commuting exactly with content.
Or each plane is a separate space attached to the core, needing no ambient
dimensions. Within a plane, a scalar on one axis keeps vector distance
equal to the difference ($0.500$ for values $0.5$ apart). A polar form,
radius as absolute time and angle as time of day, packs both components
into one vector but breaks that: for times $24$, $12$ and $0.5$ hours apart
the distances are $24.000$, $2012.000$ and $130.840$, so age must be read
from the radial part. Suggested convention: the scalar on the plane's
first axis, a cyclic companion as the angle if wanted, and planes named by
role (a time plane, a place plane), with horizontal and vertical a drawing
convention only.
\item \emph{R47, expiry as read-time exclusion.} Excluding expired cores
from $V_{\text{derived}}$, the metrics and every process makes derived
quantities functions of content and of time. The paper says every law is
deterministic, the same starting units giving the same result, so
processes need an explicit time argument $t$, defaulting to now. The
counts need a rule: stored $N$ and $Q$ against active ones. A proposal that
reuses what exists: an expired core reads as black, counted in $N$ and
absent from $Q$, $S$ and $V_{\text{derived}}$, which is the silent-contribution
rule. An atom-level expiry silences all its cores; an inner atom's expiry
must reach its cores after Collapse keeps them distinct, so either it is
copied to each core at Collapse or the grouping is kept, the minimal
reading being to copy it down at birth. A link to an expired atom is
lineage and not participation, and the proposal is that provenance stays
readable. The atom itself never changes: exclusion is a read.
\item \emph{R48, two levels.} A Collapse result carries its own new
timestamp and its cores' own, so meta vectors exist at both levels. Open:
whether the atom level also has expiry and location, and how validity nests.
\item \emph{R49, ``never lose identity.''} The word now has three senses:
value (equal content), individual (this thing with its own meta vectors),
and handle (an identifier). The ruling means that individuals persist.
Collapse keeps cores inside the result. Bundle keeps individuals as meta
vectors at the seat: value-equal ones share a seat, count is discarded as
content and survives in the labels when they differ. Mix leaves its sources
unconsumed, since it is a reading, while the result fuses their values;
UnMix stays impossible, as the paper proves the sources are not determined
by the result. So ``never lose identity'' cannot mean recoverable from the
result. It means persistence of the sources, named by the result's
backward links, and the paper should say so to avoid appearing to
contradict its own UnMix proof.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{v30 --- meta vectors need to be consistent, not meaningful}
Principle given: meta vectors are properties put to use mainly in Inference
and Assembly. They need only be consistent and mathematically accurate.
They need not make sense by themselves, as a word need not make sense by
existing; their use is extracted by context and by the path, poem or prose.

\textbf{How it sits with the paper.} It is the paper's own stance applied to
labels: a seated word does not know the poem, and the algebra ``does not
mint the relations.'' It also explains why meta vectors need no Axiom~0
admissibility, since nothing in the algebra reads them for meaning.

\textbf{What it changes in my earlier assessment.} I counted the lack of a
worked example for timestamp, expiry, location and link as a maturity gap.
Under this principle the test is consistency and accuracy, and the uses
belong to Assembly and Inference, so the remaining obligations concern
consistency only.

\textbf{What it does not remove.} ``Need not make sense'' does not mean
``may be underdetermined.'' A comparison across mixed units depends on the
unit chosen. Take $A$ at $0^\circ$ with time $0$, $B$ at $10^\circ$ with
time $3000$\,s, and $C$ at $60^\circ$ with time $100$\,s. The nearest
neighbour of $A$ is $C$ with time in seconds ($100.0$ against $3000.0$) and
$B$ with time in hours ($0.851$ against $1.000$) or in days. So any use
that compares across kinds must state its comparison rule and unit. That is
the work of the catalogue (R46) and of the comparison per kind: the vector
need not make sense, but its convention must be fixed.

\textbf{Remaining consistency obligations (R50).}
\begin{itemize}
\item The labels of a result of $T$, offset, Remove and Assembly, which the
paper states only for Collapse, Bundle and Mix. In particular, whether a
turned atom, being a new atom, gets its own new timestamp and backward
link as a Collapse result does, while its cores keep theirs.
\item Conventions that fix values: the time standard (UTC, resolution) and
the axis and unit of each scalar kind.
\item $V_{\text{derived}}$ per sort once mixed sorts are admitted (R36).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Proposed, not merged.} One sentence for Section~2.11: ``A meta
vector need not mean anything by itself. It must be well defined,
consistent, and mathematically accurate; what it is good for is extracted
by the context or the path that reads it, as a word's use is.'' If R28
splits the paper, the ``What they are for'' paragraph moves with Assembly
and Inference.
\emph{Status: principle recorded; R50 open.}


\subsection*{v31 --- the Mona Lisa again, and colour as a position}
Question: can an information atom hold an accurate, complete
representation of the Mona Lisa, as a Collapsed atom of several pure types
(colours, brush strokes, hues), each backed by a standard, the standards
being recommendations and not constraints?

\textbf{Answer, with a stand-in tested} (a $32\times32$ image and a
stroke-direction field, not the painting).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Capacity.} No limit on $N$ or $Q$ was found in the paper, and
cores may be any size.
\item \emph{Fidelity.} Lossless relative to a standard and a precision.
One core per canvas position ($N=1024$) round-tripped: the 8-bit colours
exactly, the stroke directions to $1.1\times10^{-16}$ rad, and a black
pixel as $r=0$, which is Axiom~2's empty seat. ``Complete'' can only mean
complete relative to the standards, since no finite standard exhausts a
physical painting.
\item \emph{Sense.} Not stored: a path, or a lexicon pin, reads it.
\item \emph{How it is held.} A core is a point, so one core per position,
each carrying its position as a meta vector. The Form paragraph allows a
plane, but $V_{\text{location}}$ as defined is geographic, so a canvas
position is another kind in the catalogue. Today the paper supports one
atom per sort in a bowl, aligned by position labels, which is the link
rule: criteria on other meta vectors, not content. Pairing by label was
$1024/1024$ correct and pairing by order alone $0/1024$. One atom of
several sorts needs mixed-sort cores (R36), and the Mona Lisa is the test
case for adopting it.
\item \emph{Standards.} A sort's standard attaches through the catalogue.
If one sort has several standards, sRGB or P3, someone must say which, and
decoding needs it; it might be recorded as a meta vector.
\item \emph{Cautions.} $V_{\text{derived}}$ is a summary and not a
representation, and the pixels must never be Mixed, since Mix fuses values
and UnMix is impossible whereas Collapse keeps every core.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Colour as a position (ruling).} $V_{\text{derived}}$ of colour
positions an atom in a higher space, so summaries of like colours or shades
fall in the same region. Checked:
\begin{itemize}
\item It works only if colour is carried by direction.
$V_{\text{derived}}=\sum r_i\hat n_i$ excludes $\chi$, so in the paper's
phase-colour convention, red ($\chi=0^\circ$) and green
($\chi=120^\circ$) at one direction have identical $V_{\text{derived}}$.
The colour standard must put colour in $\hat n$, while phase colour stays
for Mix arithmetic, so the paper has two colour roles to keep apart.
\item With colour carried by direction (RGB direction, strength as
brightness), dark and light red share a direction ($0.00^\circ$), a near
red is $1.31^\circ$ away, and green is $71.02^\circ$ away.
\item It is many-to-one. Fifty red with fifty green, and one hundred
uniform yellow, share the direction $(0.707,0.707,0)$, with atom coherence
$|V|/S$ of $0.707$ and $1.000$. The locator is therefore direction plus
coherence.
\item $|V_{\text{derived}}|$ scales with the number of cores ($0.519$,
$51.877$, $5187.748$ for $1$, $100$, $10000$ identical colours), so it
measures size and not colour. Position by direction and normalized
magnitudes. This also feeds the earlier caution that an uncapped $|V|$ is
heavy in the Assembly energy.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Status: R51, R52 open.}

\subsection*{v34 --- how close is v15\_28 to the larger objectives}
Objectives given: an information atom should be able to represent information
of any kind, holding, say, relativity and quantum mechanics, or ice creams and
earthquakes, without the parts interfering. More likely, atoms are separated by
the kind of their core information (weather, earthquakes, storms, paintings).
At larger scales atoms are typed and grouped by their meta vectors, so ice
creams and earthquakes can share a space if only creation time classifies
them. Free atoms should find a place in a higher space on their own accord and
gravitate to a common centroid, which is part of the goal of Assembly. The
governing philosophy: the algebra does not care where, how, or by which path or
process it is used.

\textbf{Scorecard.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Philosophy: close.} A unit carries no representation of what it
constitutes; a bowl carries no reading; levels are closed; meta vectors are
read-only and no verb changes one; determinism holds given the reading time;
names are never minted by the algebra. Leaks: the metric catalogue (about
$15\%$ of the text with Section~2.11) is read by no verb, and the enclosure is
one particular process presented inside Paper~1. What is missing is one place
that states the contract (R57).
\item \emph{Holding any kind in one atom: partly.} Close by construction:
Collapse keeps every core and merges none, Bundle is sort-blind, Tier-0 seating
makes no domain claim, and capacity is unlimited (round trip through cores was
lossless relative to a codebook, v31). Far: Axiom~7 requires one sort per atom,
so a per-vector $\tau_i$ (R36) is not adopted; $V_{\text{derived}}$ and the
metrics sum across every vector, which would interfere across sorts; and the
paper names no codebook or standard behind a core (R59). Axiom~0 limits
\emph{reading}, not \emph{holding}, and the paper does not say so in one
sentence.
\item \emph{Typing by core, grouping by meta vectors: partly.} Typing by sort
and single-sort atoms are exactly today's design. Meta vectors exist with a
stated comparison for each kind. Missing is the interface of a kind (axis, unit,
comparison rule: R46, R50), enough for any grouping to be reproducible; the
grouping procedure itself belongs to Inference and Assembly.
\item \emph{Gathering by their own accord: partly, and tested.} (Probes in
\texttt{assembly\_probe\_v15\_28.py}.) Gathering to one pile happens always,
since Assembly never repels. But (i) the pile forms at the \emph{plain mean} of
the starting seats, conserved to $7\times10^{-15}$ over random strengths and
directions (the strength-weighted mean differs from it by $0.22$ on average), so
neither content nor strength decides where; (ii) content sorts only at a
hidden scale: for $40$ atoms of two antipodal kinds, the most favourable case,
frozen at $\eta=10^{-3}$ the whole pile is $2.6\times10^{-5}$ across, the two
kinds' centres $3.3\times10^{-5}$ apart and each kind's own spread
$9.8\times10^{-6}$; a census cut of $10^{-4}$ or larger reads one mixed camp,
and only a cut of $10^{-5}$ finds about $2.7$ camps, all single-kind; (iii) seats
are given from outside, and nothing lets an atom derive its own from meta
vectors or $V_{\text{derived}}$ (R58). The paper already disclaims sorting by
strength; the same disclaimer is true of content.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Correction to my own first run.} A first experiment used
$\delta=0.05$, which is above the stability limit for $40$ atoms
($\delta<0.044$: largest stiffness eigenvalue $45.0$), and its late-time
numbers were noise. It was discarded and redone at $\delta=0.005$.

\textbf{What would close the distance, in order.} (a) State the contract in one
page (R57). (b) Decide R36 and per-sort derived quantities, the one design step
that makes ``relativity and quantum mechanics in one atom'' true and not
aspirational, with the Mona Lisa as the test (R59). (c) Give each meta-vector
kind a record of axis, unit and comparison rule (R46, R50). (d) Keep placement
an external map from meta vectors or $V_{\text{derived}}$ to a seat, which the
algebra neither supplies nor needs, and carry the gravitation story, its limits
included, to the Assembly paper, which also owns whether \emph{never repels}
survives a goal of separating kinds (R58).
\emph{Status: R57--R59 open.}


\subsection*{v35 --- the division of responsibility, and what Inference has to guess}
Stated: Paper~1 should be unaware of how atoms place themselves and gather by
content; that is the responsibility of Assembly, a higher-truth paper, while
Inference sits closer to applications, through walks, paths, processes and
transformers. If an atom contains the \emph{Principia}, it is very unlikely to
output Shakespeare; paths aimed at the \emph{Principia} (prediction,
classification) will be more successful probabilistically. A trade with noise
and some buy and sell signals should be classified or predicted by paths. Paths,
transformers and processes are formulas that must be guessed probabilistically,
since a ``Twinkle Twinkle'' path is not always readily available.

\textbf{A nuance, and a retraction of my own suggestion.} Paper~1 is not
unaware of gathering: Axiom~6 and \S10 give the pair energy and the gathering
of \emph{given} seats. It is unaware of \emph{content-driven placement}, which is
the right division. v34's suggestion that Paper~1 state the content-sorting limit,
and carry a sentence that placement is an external map, is therefore withdrawn:
Paper~1 stays silent, and the finding goes to the Assembly paper (R58). Axiom~6
and \S10 remain the one place Paper~1 touches Assembly's territory, so whether they
stay is the R28 split decision.

\textbf{What Paper~1 owes Inference.} A deterministic, replayable forward map
(given the reading time), closure (a result may be seated again), an inverse
for $T$, and composition of sequential actors into one. All are already there.
Anything more would be a use leaking into the algebra.

\textbf{Why a path has to be guessed (probes in
\texttt{inference\_probe\_v15\_28.py}, three dimensions).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{One step does not identify its actor.} For a step $x\to y$ at
angle $s$, every axis perpendicular to $x-y$ turns $x$ onto $y$ exactly
(largest error $5\times10^{-15}$ over $20{,}000$ pairs), with strength
anywhere from $s/\pi$ (axis $x\times y$) up to $1$ (a half turn about
$x+y$); for $61^\circ$, from $0.338$ to $1.000$. The phase \emph{is}
identified, $\chi_w=\chi_y-\chi_x$. The bowl's own actors are the candidates
that shrink the circle to finitely many, which is what ``paths drawn from a
bowl'' means.
\item \emph{Chance explains a step more often than it seems.} For steps
between arbitrary states, a random actor explains one to within $\rho$ with
probability equal to the cap's area fraction, $(1-\cos\rho)/2$, whatever the
strengths: $0.03\%$ at $2^\circ$, $0.19\%$ at $5^\circ$, $0.77\%$ at $10^\circ$.
A bowl of $100$ random actors explains a step to $10^\circ$ in $54\%$ of cases by
chance alone. A path counts as more successful only against that baseline,
which grows with bowl size and tolerance.
\end{itemize}
The probabilities themselves, the prior over actors, are Inference's, and
nothing in Paper~1 supplies them. \emph{Status: R60 parked for Inference; R58
now owned by Assembly.}


\subsection*{v36 --- energy stays with the atom; its use is Assembly's}
Ruling: energy is a measure of \emph{information energy} and belongs with the
atom. Its use in becoming molecules and matter is Assembly's task. This partly
settles R28: the interaction energy $U_{AB}$ and what is built on it leave
Paper~1, while $\mathcal{E}_{\text{atom}}$ and the other atom-level measures stay
in Axiom~7. It also removes the second sense of ``energy'' recorded under R56,
since $\mathcal{E}$ would be the only one left.

\textbf{A check that supports the split.} $U_{AB}=-C_{AB}\,g(d)$ with a coupling
$C_{AB}=\tfrac12 r_Ar_B(3+\hat n_A\!\cdot\!\hat n_B)$ and a kernel
$g=(d^2+\varepsilon_{AB}^2)^{-3/2}$ whose softening $\varepsilon_{AB}=r_A+r_B$
also comes from the atoms; only $d$ comes from outside. At contact the energy
therefore depends on the atoms alone, but it is not a well-behaved atom
property: for two aligned atoms of equal strength $r$ it is $-(3+1)/(16r)$,
which is $-0.278$ at $r=0.9$, $-25.0$ at $r=0.01$ and $-250$ at $r=0.001$,
and it jumps to exactly $0$ at black. $\mathcal{E}_{\text{atom}}$ is bounded and
continuous at zero. So the atom keeps $\mathcal{E}$; the meaning of a pair
energy is fixed only by seats and a process, which is use.

\subsection*{v38 --- mixed atoms decided: one atom, several sorts, a derived vector at each level}
Ruling: a Collapsed atom is fundamentally a denser atom holding several sorts.
The new atom has one meta vector $V_{\text{derived}}$, calculated on the new
information combining all its pure sorts; each pure atom keeps its own
$V_{\text{derived}}$, for that atom and for its core; every atom has its own
set of meta vectors. The bowl is another representation of a Collapsed atom.

\textbf{Checked against v15\_28.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The paper already defines it this way.} $V_{\text{derived}}$ exists
per core and per atom, the atom's is exactly the sum of its cores', and $\tau$
does not enter the sum, so over mixed sorts the whole-atom value is defined as
written. Checked on an atom of two colour cores and three word cores: the
whole equals the colour atom's plus the word atom's, equals the sum of the
five cores' own, and UnCollapsing the word cores returns the colour atom's
value.
\item \emph{It supersedes my v19 record} that cross-sort derived quantities are
undefined and computed per sort until a rule exists, and R36's row text. It
agrees with v30: $V_{\text{derived}}$ is a meta vector, needs no admissibility
and no sense of its own, and Axiom~0 is untouched because Collapse still
juxtaposes and mints no relation. One caveat: the sum mixes the addresses of
different lexicons, so two systems agree on it only if they share the same $E_L$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Left to settle.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Reading a constituent's $V_{\text{derived}}$ from the result alone.}
The paper provides persistence: sources are not consumed, the result names them
by a backward link, and each core keeps its own $V_{\text{derived}}$. Recovering a
constituent atom's value from the result alone would need a core-level source
label, which is the identity handle of R18.
\item \emph{The bowl as a representation needs a precise reading.} It is exact
for a counted bowl; the paper's bowl is a set, the distinct-core view.
For $A\oplus B\oplus A$ ($N=3$, $D=2$), atom to bowl to atom returns $N=2$ and
loses the $N-D=1$ repeat, while bowl to atom to bowl is the identity. This
matters for Mix: one shell of strength $0.4$ reads $r'=0.40$ in the set view,
and four identical shells counted read $r'=1.00$. The remaining fork is
whether Mix reads a Collapsed atom with its counts (R62, R39); Inference v2
\S8.1 currently says it does, as a reading it adds.
\item \emph{The seven metrics.} The ruling names $V_{\text{derived}}$ only; whether
the metrics follow the same two levels is R43, narrowed.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What changes in the paper when this is merged.} Axiom~7's requirement of
